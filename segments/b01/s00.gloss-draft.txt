ANALYSIS
1-9: Lat: [voc.] Aeneadum genetrix… alma Venus; rel. quae (Venus, 2sg) concelebras mare, terras; causal quoniam genus omne concipitur (pres. pass.) visitque lumina solis (exortum → genus); main: te fugiunt venti, (fugiunt) nubila caeli, obj. te adventumque tuum; tibi tellus summittit flores (suavis → flores/tellus); tibi rident aequora; caelum placatum nitet lumine diffuso. Eng: same vocative; who (2sg) fill/throng sea, lands; since every kind is conceived, looks on light; winds, clouds flee you and your coming; earth sends up flowers; seas laugh; sky made calm shines. MATCH (concelebras as two verbs, compression).
10-13: Lat: simul ac species verna diei patefactast et aura favoni reserata viget; volucres (perculsae corda tua vi) significant te tuumque initum. Eng: as soon as face of spring day revealed and west wind's breath, unbarred, grows strong; birds announce you and your coming in, hearts struck by your power. MATCH.
15,14,16: Lat: ferae pecudes persultant pabula et tranant amnis; ita (quaeque) capta lepore te sequitur quo quamque inducere pergis. Eng: wild beasts and herds bound, swim; each, captured, follows you wherever you go on to lead. MATCH (quamque absorbed into "each").
17-20: Lat: (tu) incutiens amorem omnibus efficis ut saecla cupide generatim propagent. Eng: striking love into all, you make them propagate their generations kind by kind. MATCH.
21-27: Lat: quoniam (tu) sola gubernas naturam rerum, nec quicquam exoritur sine te neque fit laetum/amabile; studeo te sociam esse versibus, quos pango Memmiadae, quem tu voluisti excellere ornatum omnibus rebus. Eng: since you alone steer…, nothing rises, nothing comes to be glad/lovely; I long for you as partner in verses I set down for Memmius, whom you willed to excel, adorned. MATCH.
28: Lat: quo magis da (impv.) dictis leporem aeternum. Eng: all the more give my words lasting charm. MATCH.
29-37: Lat: effice ut moenera militiai sopita quiescant; nam tu sola potes iuvare mortalis pace, quoniam Mavors regit moenera belli, qui se reiicit in gremium tuum devictus vulnere, suspiciens pascit visus amore, spiritus (eius resupini) pendet ex ore tuo. Eng: same clauses, Mars subject throughout, breath hangs on your lips. MATCH.
38-43: Lat: hunc recubantem tu circumfusa (ptc.) funde loquellas petens pacem Romanis; nam neque nos possumus agere hoc aequo animo nec (potest) propago desse saluti. Eng: pour yourself around him (impv.), pour out speech asking peace for Romans; neither can we work untroubled, nor can Memmius be wanting to common safety. MATCH (participle made coordinate imperative, sense kept).
44-46: Lat: necessest omnis divum natura fruatur aevo cum pace, semota ab rebus nostris. Eng: whole nature of gods must enjoy immortal life in peace, removed from our affairs. MATCH.
47-49: Lat: (natura) privata dolore, periclis, pollens opibus, indiga nihil, nec capitur bene promeritis nec tangitur ira. Eng: free from pain, dangers, strong, needing nothing; neither won by services nor touched by anger. MATCH.
50-53: Lat: adhibe (impv., tu) auris animumque ad veram rationem, ne (tu) relinquas contempta dona mea intellecta prius quam sint. Eng: lend ears and mind to true reasoning, so that my gifts are not dismissed and left behind (passive, no agent). DIFF: 2sg agent of relinquas (you scorn and abandon) removed.
54-61: Lat: nam disserere incipiam de summa ratione caeli deumque et pandam primordia, unde natura creet res, quove natura resolvat (res) peremptas; quae nos suemus vocare materiem, genitalia corpora, semina rerum, usurpare corpora prima, quod ex illis sunt omnia. Eng: same chain; relative "which we are used to calling…" on first-beginnings. MATCH.
62-71: Lat: cum vita iaceret oppressa sub religione, quae ostendebat caput instans mortalibus, Graius homo primum est ausus tollere oculos et obsistere; quem neque fama… nec caelum compressit, sed (ea) inritat virtutem, ut cupiret effringere claustra naturae. Eng: same; heaven held him down not; rather they goaded his courage so he longed to break gates. MATCH.
72-77: Lat: vis animi pervicit, processit extra moenia, peragravit immensum; unde refert quid possit oriri, quid nequeat, qua ratione cuique sit potestas finita et terminus haerens. Eng: same. MATCH.
78-79: Lat: religio subiecta pedibus opteritur; victoria exaequat nos caelo. Eng: same. MATCH.
80-82: Lat: vereor ne (tu) rearis te inire elementa impia viamque indugredi sceleris. Eng: I fear you might think you enter on impious elements and set foot on road of crime. MATCH.
82-83: Lat: contra illa religio peperit facta scelerosa. Eng: religion has borne criminal deeds. MATCH.
84-86: Lat: quo pacto ductores turparunt aram sanguine Iphianassai. Eng: passive, altar defiled by the leaders. MATCH.
87-92: Lat: cui simul infula profusast et (illa) sensit parentem adstare, ministros celare ferrum, civis lacrimas effundere aspectu suo; muta metu, summissa genibus terram petebat. Eng: same; she saw; she sank to ground. MATCH.
93-94: Lat: nec quibat prodesse quod princeps donarat regem nomine patrio. Eng: same. MATCH.
95-100: Lat: sublata, tremibunda deductast, non ut posset comitari Hymenaeo more perfecto, sed ut hostia concideret mactatu parentis, ut exitus daretur classi. Eng: same. MATCH.
101: Lat: religio potuit suadere tantum malorum. Eng: same. MATCH.
102-103: Lat: tutemet victus dictis vatum quaeres desciscere a nobis. Eng: same. MATCH.
104-106: Lat: quam multa somnia (vates) fingere possunt, quae possint vertere rationes vitae, turbare fortunas. Eng: same. MATCH.
107-109: Lat: si homines viderent finem aerumnarum, valerent obsistere religionibus minisque. Eng: same. MATCH.
110-111: Lat: nunc nulla ratio restandi, quoniam timendum (est) poenas. Eng: same. MATCH.
112-119: Lat: ignoratur quae sit natura animai, nata sit an insinuetur nascentibus et intereat nobiscum an visat tenebras an insinuet se pecudes alias, ut Ennius cecinit, qui detulit coronam quae clueret. Eng: same. MATCH.
120-123: Lat: etsi Ennius exponit Acherusia templa esse, quo neque animae neque corpora permaneant, sed simulacra. Eng: same. MATCH.
124-126: Lat: unde (Ennius) commemorat speciem Homeri exortam coepisse lacrimas effundere et naturam expandere. Eng: same. MATCH.
127-135: Lat: cum habenda est ratio de superis rebus, qua ratione fiant meatus, qua vi gerantur, tunc videndum unde constet anima animique natura, et quae res terrificet mentes, uti videamur cernere eos quorum ossa tellus amplectitur. Eng: same. MATCH.
136-145: Lat: nec me fallit difficile esse inlustrare reperta, cum sit agendum verbis novis; sed virtus et voluptas suadet efferre laborem et inducit vigilare quaerentem quibus dictis possim praepandere lumina, quibus possis convisere res. Eng: same. MATCH.
FINDINGS
53	GRAMMAR	MODERATE	0.60	"contempta relinquas" has Memmius as the agent who would scorn and abandon the gifts, but the English passive "are not … dismissed and left behind" removes the second-person agent the register requires to be kept; suggested fix: "you do not, before you understand them, scorn and leave behind."
