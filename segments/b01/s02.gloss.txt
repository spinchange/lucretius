464-470

471-482
L: si nulla materies fuisset nec locus ac spatium, numquam ignis conflatus amore (forma Tyndaridis) gliscens sub pectore Alexandri accendisset certamina belli, nec equos durateus clam Troiianis inflammasset Pergama partu nocturno Graiiugenarum; ut possis perspicere res gestas omnis funditus non constare neque esse per se ut corpus, nec cluere eadem ratione qua inane, sed ut possis vocare eventa corporis atque loci.
E: without matter and place, never would the fire kindled by love glowing in Alexander's breast have lit the war, nor the wooden horse, unknown to the Trojans, have set Pergamum ablaze by its night delivery of Greeks; so that you may see all deeds, from the ground up, do not exist by themselves as body, nor on the principle of void, but are accidents of body and place.
