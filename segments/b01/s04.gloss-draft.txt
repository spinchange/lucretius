ANALYSIS
599-608: Lat: since there is (est) an outermost point (cacumen) of that body which our senses cannot (nequeunt) see, it (id) stands (extat) without parts and consists (constat) of least nature, and neither was (fuit) separate nor will be able (valebit) to be, since it is itself (ipsum) a part of another, first and one; then (inde) other parts fill out (explent) the body's nature; since these cannot (nequeunt) stand by themselves, they must cling (necessest haerere) where they can (queant) by no principle be torn away. Eng: same. MATCH
609-614: Lat: first-beginnings are (sunt) of solid singleness, which cohere (cohaerent), packed (stipata) with least parts, not combined (conciliata) from their gathering, but strong (pollentia) in eternal singleness; from which (unde) nature does not allow (neque... concedit) anything to be torn away or diminished, keeping (reservans) seeds for things. Eng: same; the negation sits on avelli/deminui ("nothing can be torn away... so nature allows"). MATCH
615-618: Lat: unless there is (erit) a least, the smallest bodies will consist (constabunt) of infinite parts, since the half of a half will always have (habebit) a half, nor will anything set a limit (praefiniet). Eng: same. MATCH
619-622: Lat: therefore between the sum and the least there will be (erit) nothing to differ (distet); for although (quamvis) the whole sum is (sit) infinite, the smallest things will consist (constabunt) of infinite parts just the same. Eng: same. MATCH
623-627: Lat: since true reasoning cries out (reclamat) and denies (negat) the mind can believe it, you must admit (fateare necessest) that there are things (esse ea) which stand (extent) with no parts and consist (constent) of least nature; since these are (sunt), you must admit (fatendum) that they too are solid and eternal. Eng: same. MATCH
628-634: Lat: if nature, the creator, had been accustomed (consuesset) to force (cogere) all things to be broken up (resolvi) into least parts, it (eadem) could restore (valeret reparare) nothing from them, because things that have no parts cannot (non possunt) have what generative matter must (debet) have: connections, weights, blows, collisions, motions, by which each thing is carried on (geruntur). Eng: same. MATCH
635-637: Lat: those who thought (putarunt) matter is fire and the sum consists (consistere) of fire alone seem (videntur) to have slipped (lapsi). Eng: same. MATCH
638-640: Lat: Heraclitus, their leader (dux), first enters (init) the battle, famous (clarus) for dark speech more among the empty than among the serious Greeks who seek (requirunt) the truth. Eng: same. MATCH
641-644: Lat: fools (stolidi) admire and love (admirantur amantque) more (magis) all things which they see (cernunt) lurking (latitantia) under inverted words, and set up as true (constituunt) what can (possunt) prettily touch the ears and is painted (fucata) with charming sound. Eng: same. MATCH
645-646: Lat: why could things (possent) be so various, I ask (requiro), if they are created (creatae) from one pure fire? Eng: same. MATCH
647-649: Lat: it would profit (prodesset) nothing for fire to be made dense (denserier) or rare, if the parts had (haberent) the same nature as the whole fire. Eng: same. MATCH
650-651: Lat: heat would be (esset) fiercer with parts drawn together (abl. abs.), fainter with them scattered. Eng: same. MATCH
652-654: Lat: there is nothing more you could suppose (rearis) could happen, much less (ne dum) that such variety could (queat) arise from dense and rare fires. Eng: same. MATCH
655-656: Lat: if they were to make (faciant) void mixed in, fires could (poterunt) be made dense and left rare. Eng: same. MATCH
657-664: Lat: because they see (cernunt) many things contrary to them (sibi) and shrink (fugitant) from leaving pure void, fearing (metuunt) the steep, they lose (amittunt) the true path, nor do they see (cernunt) that with void removed (abl. abs.) all things are made dense and become one body, which could send (possit) nothing swiftly, as fire throws (iacit) light and heat, so that you may see (videas) [fire] is not of packed parts. Eng: same. MATCH
665-669: Lat: if they believe (credunt) fires can be quenched (stingui) and change (mutare) their body in union, if, that is, they will refrain (reparcent) from doing it in no part, the heat will perish (occidet) to nothing and whatever is created will come (fient) from nothing. Eng: same. MATCH
670-674: refrain. MATCH
675-679: Lat: since there are (sunt) certain bodies that keep (conservant) their nature, by whose departure, arrival or changed order things change (mutant) nature and transform (convertunt) [their] bodies, you may know (scire licet) these bodies are not fiery. Eng: same. MATCH
680-683: Lat: it would make no difference (referret) for some to withdraw, others to be added, others to change order, if all kept (tenerent) the nature of heat; whatever they created (crearet) would be (foret) fire. Eng: same. MATCH
684-689: Lat: there are (sunt) bodies whose collisions etc. make (efficiunt) fires and, order changed, change (mutant) nature, and [the bodies] are not (neque sunt) like fire or any other thing that could (possit) send bodies to the senses and touch our touch. Eng: same. MATCH
690-692: Lat: to say (dicere) all things are fire and nothing true exists except fire, as he does (facit), seems (videtur) mad. Eng: same. MATCH
693-695: Lat: he himself (ipse) fights (repugnat) against the senses from the senses and shakes (labefactat) them, on which all beliefs depend (pendent), from which the fire he names became known (cognitus est) to him. Eng: same. MATCH
696-697: Lat: he believes (credit) the senses know fire truly, does not believe (non credit) the rest, which are no less clear. Eng: same. MATCH
698-700: Lat: this seems (videtur) both empty and mad; to what shall we appeal (referemus)? What can be (potest) surer than the senses, by which we may mark (notemus) true and false? Eng: same. MATCH
701-704: Lat: why should anyone rather do away with (tollat) all things and wish (velit) to leave heat alone, than deny (neget) fires exist yet leave (relinquat) another nature? it seems (videtur) equal madness to say either. Eng: same. MATCH
705-711: Lat: those who thought fire is matter and the sum can (posse) consist of fire, and who set up (constituere) air, or who thought water shapes (fingere) things or earth creates (creare) all and is turned (vertier) into all natures, seem (videntur) to have strayed (derrasse) far. Eng: same. MATCH
712-715: Lat: add those who double (conduplicant) the first-beginnings, joining (iungentes) air to fire, earth to water, and who think (rentur) all can grow (procrescere) from four things. Eng: same. MATCH
716-721: Lat: foremost (cum primis) of these is Empedocles, whom the island bore (gessit), which the Ionian sea flowing around (fluitans) sprinkles (aspargit) with brine, and whose narrow strait divides (dividit) the shores of Aeolia's lands from its borders. Eng: same. MATCH
722-725: Lat: here is (est) Charybdis, and here Etna's rumblings threaten (minantur) that the angers gather (colligere) again, so that its force may vomit (vomat) fires and carry (ferat) lightning to the sky. Eng: same. MATCH
726-730: Lat: although (cum) the region seems (videtur) wondrous and is said (fertur) worth seeing, rich and fortified, it seems (videtur) to have had (habuisse) nothing more glorious, holy, wondrous, dear than this man. Eng: same. MATCH
731-733: Lat: his songs cry out (vociferantur) and set forth (exponunt) discoveries, so that he scarcely seems (videatur) born of human stock. Eng: same. MATCH
734-741: Lat: he and those above, [who are] inferior and lesser, although discovering (invenientes) much, and giving (dedere) answers holier and surer than the Pythia who speaks (profatur), made (fecere) ruin in the beginnings and fell (cecidere) heavily. Eng: same; inferiores attached to the earlier group, not to Empedocles. MATCH
742-745: Lat: first, they set up (constituunt) motion with void removed (abl. abs.) and leave (relinquunt) things soft and rare, and yet do not mix (nec admiscent) void into their body. Eng: same. MATCH
746-752: Lat: next, they will make (facient) no limit to cutting, no halt to breaking, nothing least to exist, when we see (videamus) that the outermost point of each thing is (esse) what seems (videtur) least to our senses, so that you can infer (possis) that the outermost point of things you cannot see (quis) stands (consistere) as least. Eng: same. MATCH
753-758: Lat: there is added (accedit) that, since they make (constituunt) the first-beginnings soft, which we see (videmus) are born and mortal, [it follows] that the sum must (debeat) return to nothing and the store grow strong (vigescere) reborn from nothing; you will grasp (habebis) how far each stands (distet) from truth. Eng: same. MATCH
759-762: Lat: they are (sunt) hostile and a poison to each other; therefore either, having met (congressa), they will perish (peribunt), or will scatter (diffugient) as we see (videmus) thunderbolts, rains, winds scatter when a storm is gathered (abl. abs.). Eng: same. MATCH
