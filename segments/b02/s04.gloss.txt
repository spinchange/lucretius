ANALYSIS
581-585: L: [it] convenit (pres ind) + habere obsignatum / tenere mandatum (inf); content: nil esse (inf) [quorum natura videtur in promptu] quod consistat (subj) genere ex uno principiorum; nec quicquam [esse] quod non constet (subj, neg) permixto semine. E: it is fitting to keep sealed / to hold entrusted: nothing whose nature is seen, that consists of one kind; nothing that is not made of mingled seed. MATCH.
586-588: L: quod cumque possidet (pres) magis vis multas atque potestates, ita [id] docet (pres) esse in sese plurima genera ac varias figuras. E: whatever possesses more forces and powers, the more it shows it holds most kinds and most varied shapes. MATCH.
589-591: L: tellus habet corpora prima, unde fontes (subj) volventes frigora renovent (subj) mare inmensum; habet [corpora] unde ignes oriantur. E: earth holds first bodies from which springs, rolling coolness, renew the sea; holds those from which fires rise. MATCH.
592-593: L: nam sola terrae succensa ardent (pl) multis locis; impetus Aetnae furit ex imis ignibus. E: for the ground is kindled and burns in many places; from its depths Etna's onrush rages with fire. MATCH (vero unrendered, negligible).
594-597: L: [tellus] habet unde possit extollere fruges arbustaque gentibus humanis; unde possit praebere fluvios frondes pabula generi ferarum. E: it has the means to raise crops and orchards for men; means to offer rivers, leaves, pastures to the beasts. MATCH.
598-599: L: quare haec una dicta est (perf pass) magna deum mater, mater ferarum, genetrix corporis nostri. E: therefore she alone has been called mother of gods, of beasts, begetter of our body. MATCH.
600-603: L: veteres docti poetae Graium cecinere (perf) hanc agitare (inf) biiugos leones sedibus in curru, docentes tellurem pendere in spatio aeris neque posse terram sistere in terra. E: the learned poets of old sang that, seated in a chariot, she drives a yoke of lions, teaching that earth hangs in air and cannot rest on earth. MATCH.
604-605: L: adiunxere feras, quia proles quamvis effera debet molliri (pass inf) victa officiis parentum. E: they yoked beasts because offspring, however wild, must be softened, won by parents' kindnesses. MATCH.
606-607: L: cinxere caput corona murali, quia [ea] munita eximiis locis sustinet urbes. E: they bound her head with a crown of walls because, fortified in choice places, she bears up cities. MATCH.
608-609: L: imago divinae matris praedita quo insigni fertur (pass) horrifice per terras. E: adorned with this emblem, the image is carried through the lands, inspiring dread. MATCH.
610-613: L: gentes vocitant hanc Idaeam matrem, dant Phrygias catervas comites, quia edunt (they declare) fruges coepisse creari primum ex illis finibus. E: peoples call her Idaean Mother, give Phrygian bands as companions, because, they say, crops first began to be created from those regions. MATCH.
614-617: L: Gallos attribuunt, quia volunt significare [eos] qui violarint numen Matris et inventi sint ingrati genitoribus, putandos esse indignos qui edant progeniem in oras luminis. E: they assign Galli because they wish to signify that those who outraged the Mother's power and were ungrateful to parents must be thought unworthy to bring forth offspring into the shores of light. MATCH.
618-623: L: tympana tonant palmis, cymbala circum, cornua minantur, tibia stimulat mentis; [they] praeportant tela, signa furoris, quae (= tela) possint conterrere animos ac pectora volgi numine divae. E: drums thunder, cymbals, horns threaten, pipe goads minds; they carry weapons, signs of frenzy, so that minds and hearts of the crowd may be terrified by the goddess's power. DIFF (minor): the relative clause whose subject is the weapons becomes a passive purpose clause; sense of intent kept, weapons' agency implicit.
624-628: L: cum primum [ea] invecta per urbes munificat (pres) mortalis tacita salute muta, sternunt iter aere atque argento, ditantes stipe, ninguntque floribus rosarum, umbrantes matrem catervamque. E: when she is carried in and silently enriches mortals with a wordless blessing, they strew the road with bronze and silver, enriching it with alms, and snow down roses, shading mother and band. MATCH.
629-639: L: hic armata manus, quos Grai memorant Curetas, si forte ludunt et exultant laeti sanguine, quatientes cristas numine, referunt Curetas Dictaeos, qui feruntur occultasse vagitum Iovis, cum pueri circum puerum pulsarent (impf subj) aera aeribus, ne Saturnus adeptus mandaret eum malis daretque matri volnus. E: an armed troop whom Greeks call Curetes, when they sport and leap, glad of blood, shaking crests with nodding, recall the Dictaean Curetes, said to have hidden Jupiter's wailing, when boys around the boy beat bronze on bronze, so that Saturn might not catch him, give him to his jaws, and wound the Mother. MATCH (636 garbled line rendered as printed).
640-643: L: propterea armati comitantur matrem, aut quia significant divam praedicere ut velint defendere patriam armis ac virtute parentque esse praesidio decorique parentibus. E: therefore they attend her armed, or because they signify the goddess proclaims that men should be willing to defend their land and make ready to be protection and honor to parents. MATCH.
644-645: L: quae, quamvis ferantur disposta bene, sunt tamen repulsa longe a vera ratione. E: these, however well set out, are driven far from true reasoning. MATCH.
646-651: L: omnis divom natura necessest fruatur (subj) aevo inmortali cum pace, semota seiunctaque; nam privata dolore periclis, pollens opibus, indiga nihil nostri, nec capitur promeritis neque tangitur ira. E: refrain of 1.44-49: the whole nature of the gods must enjoy immortal life in peace, removed; free from pain and dangers, strong, needing nothing, neither won by services nor touched by anger. MATCH.
652-654: L: terra caret sensu omni tempore, et quia potitur primordia multarum rerum, effert multa modis multis in lumina solis. E: earth is at all times without sense, and because it possesses first-beginnings of many things, brings forth many in many ways into sunlight. MATCH.
655-659, 680: L: siquis constituet (fut) vocare mare Neptunum fruges Cererem et mavolt abuti nomine Bacchi quam proferre vocamen proprium, concedamus ut dictitet orbem esse deum matrem, dum ipse vera re parcat contingere animum religione turpi. E: if anyone resolves to call sea Neptune, crops Ceres, would rather misuse Bacchus' name than speak the proper word, let us grant he may keep declaring the earth mother of the gods, if only in truth he refrains from tainting his mind with foul religion. MATCH.
660-665: L: pecudes, proles equorum, greges, tondentes ex uno campo, sedantes sitim ex uno flumine, vivont dissimili specie retinentque naturam parentum et quaeque imitantur mores generatim. E: flocks, horses, herds, cropping one field, quenching thirst from one stream, live unlike in appearance, keep parents' nature, each kind by kind copies their ways. MATCH.
666-667: L: tanta est dissimilis ratio materiai in quovis genere herbae, tanta in flumine. E: so great is the unlike order of matter in any grass, so great in any river. MATCH.
668-671: L: ossa cruor... nervi constituunt quamvis animantem unam, quae sunt distantia longe, perfecta dissimili figura principiorum. E: bones, blood... sinews make up any one creature, which are far distant, formed of unlike shapes of beginnings. MATCH.
672-675: L: quaecumque flammata cremantur, si nil praeterea, tamen tradunt haec in corpore, unde possint iacere ignem, submittere lumen, agere scintillas, differre favillam. E: all things set aflame, if nothing else, carry these in their body: the means to throw fire, send up light, drive off sparks, scatter ash. MATCH.
676-678: L: peragrans cetera consimili ratione mentis invenies (fut) [ea] celare semina multarum rerum corpore et cohibere figuras. E: going through the rest by like reasoning, you will find they hide seeds of many things in their body and contain shapes. MATCH.
679, 681: L: vides multa quibus color et sapor reddita sunt una cum odore, in primis poma. E: you see many things in which color and taste together are given with smell; first, most fruits. MATCH.
682: L: haec debent constare variis figuris. E: these must consist of varied shapes. MATCH.
683-685: L: nidor penetrat qua fucus non it in artus; fucus sorsum, sapor sorsum insinuatur sensibus; ut noscas [ea] differre primis figuris. E: smell gets into our limbs where color does not; color apart, taste apart works into senses; so you may know they differ in first shapes. MATCH.
686-687: L: formae dissimiles conveniunt in glomeramen unum et res constant permixto semine. E: unlike forms come together into one mass; things are made of mingled seed. MATCH.
688-694: L: vides elementa communia multis verbis, cum necesse est confiteare versus ac verba constare ex aliis elementis; non quo multa parum littera communis currat aut nulla duo sint ex omnibus isdem, sed quia non volgo omnia constant paria omnibus. E: you see many elements common to many words, though you must admit verses and words are made from different elements; not because few letters run in common or no two are made of letters entirely the same, but because not all are alike to all. MATCH.
695-699: L: cum communia multa sint multarum rerum, primordia possunt consistere dissimili summa inter se; ut feratur merito humanum genus et fruges constare ex aliis. E: since many are common to many things, first-beginnings can make up unlike wholes; so that it may be said men and crops are made of different things. MATCH.
700-706: L: nec putandum est omnia posse conecti omnimodis; nam videres (impf subj, unreal) portenta fieri, species semiferas existere, ramos egigni corpore vivo, membra conecti marinis, naturam pascere Chimaeras per terras omniparentis. E: we must not think all can be joined in every way; you would see monsters come to be, half-beast forms spring up, branches from a living body, land limbs joined to sea, Chimaeras, and nature feeding them. MATCH.
707-709: L: manifestum est nil quorum fieri, quando videmus omnia creata seminibus certis genetrice certa posse conservare genus crescentia. E: plain that none happens, since all things created from fixed seeds and mother can, we see, preserve their kind as they grow. MATCH.
710: L: scilicet necessust id fieri certa ratione. E: clearly this must come about by a fixed principle. MATCH.
711-717: L: nam corpora sua cuique discedunt ex cibis in artus et conexa efficiunt motus convenientis; at videmus naturam reicere aliena in terras, multaque caecis corporibus fugiunt e corpore percita plagis, quae neque potuere conecti neque consentire atque imitari motus vitalis. E: to each its own bodies pass into the limbs and, joined, produce fitting motions; we see nature cast out the alien, and many things in unseen bodies flee the body, driven by blows, which could neither be joined nor feel with and imitate the vital motions. MATCH.
718-719: L: ne putes animalia sola teneri legibus his, quaedam ratio terminat res omnis. E: lest you think only living things are held by these laws, a certain principle bounds all things. MATCH.
720-724: L: nam uti res quaeque genitae sunt dissimiles inter se tota natura, ita necessest quamque constare dissimili figura principiorum; non quo... sed quia... (refrain). E: as things begotten are unlike in whole nature, so each must be made of unlike shapes of beginnings; refrain verbatim. MATCH.
725-729: L: cum semina distent, necessust differre intervalla... motus; quae non solum seiungunt corpora animalia sed secernunt terras ac mare caelumque retentant a terris. E: since seeds differ, there must be differences in intervals... motions, which not only divide living bodies but keep lands and sea apart and hold heaven back from the lands. MATCH.
