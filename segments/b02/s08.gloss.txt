ANALYSIS

**1105-1111**
- Latin: corpora multa addita sunt (pf. pass. pl.) extrinsecus post tempus genitale mundi diemque primigenum… coortum[que] solis; addita [sunt] circum semina; rel. quae (obj.) contulit (pf.) magnum omne (subj.) iaculando; unde mare et terrae possent augescere (impf. subj., purpose/potential); et unde appareret spatium (subj. of appareret); caeli domus (nom., subj.) tolleret alta tecta procul a terris; et consurgeret aer.
- English: many bodies were added from outside after birth-time / first-born day / rising of sun; seeds added round about, which the great whole brought together by hurling; from which sea and lands could increase; from which "the space of heaven's house" could appear and lift its roof; air rise up.
- DIFF: caeli domus (nominative, subject of tolleret) is made a genitive on spatium, so "space" lifts the roof.

**1112-1117**
- Latin: omnia corpora distribuuntur (pres. pass.) plagis sua cuique ex omnibus locis, et recedunt ad sua saecla; umor [recedit] ad umorem; terra crescit terreno corpore; ignes procudunt ignem; aether [procudit] aethera; donique natura creatrix, perfica, perduxit (pf.) omnia ad extremum finem crescendi.
- English: same, with "bringing all to completion" for perfica.
- MATCH

**1118-1119**
- Latin: ut fit ubi nihilo plus est quod datur intra venas quam quod fluit atque recedit.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**1120-1121**
- Latin: hic aetas debet consistere omnibus rebus; hic natura refrenat auctum suis viribus.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**1122-1127**
- Latin: quae cumque vides grandescere… scandere, [ea] adsumunt plura corpora quam mittunt; dum cibus omnis (nom., with cibus) facile inditur in venas; dum non ita late dispessa sunt ut multa remittant et plus dispendi faciant quam vescitur aetas.
- English: whatever things you see… take more bodies than they send; while food is easily put into "all their veins"; while not spread so widely…
- DIFF: omnis is moved from cibus to venas.

**1128-1130**
- Latin: manus dandum est (gerundive, "we must grant") multa corpora fluere atque recedere rebus; sed plura debent accedere, donec tetigere (pf.) summum cacumen alescendi.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**1131-1132**
- Latin: inde aetas minutatim frangit vires et robur adultum, et liquitur in partem peiorem.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**1133-1138**
- Latin: quanto res amplior est (augmine adempto, abl. abs.) et quo latior, plura corpora dispargit et mittit; nec cibus omnis (with cibus) facile diditur ei in venas; nec satis est, pro quam largos aestus exaestuat, unde queat tantum suboriri ac subpeditare.
- English: same, but "distributed into all its veins".
- DIFF: omnis is again moved from cibus to venas.

**1139-1143**
- Latin: iure igitur pereunt, cum rarefacta sunt fluendo et cum omnia succumbunt externis plagis; quando cibus denique defit grandi aevo; nec corpora tuditantia cessant extrinsecus ullam rem conficere et domare plagis, infesta.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**1144-1149**
- Latin: sic moenia mundi quoque, expugnata, dabunt (fut.) labem putrisque ruinas; enim cibus debet integrare, fulcire, sustentare omnia, ne quiquam, quoniam nec venae perpetiuntur quod satis est, neque natura ministrat quantum opus est.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**1150-1152**
- Latin: iam aetas fracta est, tellusque effeta vix creat parva animalia, quae (subj.) creavit cuncta saecla deditque partu ingentia corpora ferarum.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**1153-1156**
- Latin: haud funis aurea (subj.) demisit (pf.) mortalia saecla (obj.) de caelo in arva; nec mare nec fluctus crearunt; sed tellus genuit, eadem quae nunc alit ex se.
- English: same, inverted order.
- MATCH

**1157-1163**
- Latin: ipsa (tellus) creavit sponte sua primum fruges, vineta…; dedit fetus, pabula; quae nunc vix grandescunt aucta labore; conterimus boves et viris agricolarum; conficimus ferrum, [nos] vix arvis suppeditati (masc. pl., with "we"); usque adeo parcunt fetus augentque laborem.
- English: same, but "the iron share, scarcely supplied by the fields".
- DIFF: suppeditati (we) now reads as modifying the share.

**1164-1167**
- Latin: arator suspirat crebrius [se] in cassum cecidisse labores; cum confert praesentia tempora praeteritis, laudat fortunas parentis.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**1168-1172**
- Latin: sator vitis incusat momen temporis saeclumque fatigat, et crepat ut genus antiquum… tolerarit (pf. subj.) aevom angustis finibus, cum (concessive) modus agri esset multo minor viritim.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**1173-1174**
- Latin: nec tenet omnia paulatim tabescere et ire ad capulum, defessa (with omnia) spatio vetusto aetatis.
- English: same.
- MATCH

FINDINGS
1125	GRAMMAR	MINOR	0.55	"cibus omnis" (all the food) has omnis moved to venas, "into all their veins"; "while all the food is easily put into their veins, and while"
1110	GRAMMAR	MODERATE	0.50	"appareret spatium caeli domus altaque tecta / tolleret" has caeli domus as nominative subject of tolleret, but the English makes it a genitive ("the space of heaven's house") and makes space lift the roof; "room could appear, and heaven's house lift its lofty roof"
1136	GRAMMAR	MINOR	0.50	"cibus omnis diditur" again has omnis moved from cibus to venas, "into all its veins"; "nor is all the food easily distributed into its veins,"
1162	AMBIGUITY	MINOR	0.50	"vix arvis suppeditati" (masc. pl., "we, scarcely supplied by the fields") is placed so it reads as modifying "the iron share"; "we wear down the iron, we whom the fields scarcely supply:"
