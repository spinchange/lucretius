ANALYSIS

973-975
Latin: [purpose] uti possint sentire animalia quaeque (subj. animalia quaeque, pres. subj. pl.); [cond.] si sensus tribuendus est principiis eorum (gerundive, pres.); [main question] quid (de principiis) de quibus genus humanum propritim auctumst (perf. pass., subj. genus).
English: so that each kind of living creature can feel; if sense must be granted to their beginnings; what of those from which the human race is properly made.
DIFF: "quaeque" (each creature) becomes "each kind"; "propritim" (in particular) becomes "properly".

976-979
Latin: subject is the implied principia; concussa cachinnant, spargunt ora genasque, callent dicere, quaerunt quae sint primordia (all pres. ind. pl.; scilicet ironic).
English: they must shake and guffaw, sprinkle, be skilled at saying, ask what their first-beginnings are.
MATCH ("must" carries the irony of scilicet).

980-982
Latin: [causal] adsimulata (modifying the principia) debent constare ex aliis elementis; inde alia ex aliis; [result] ut nusquam consistere ausis (2nd sg. subj.).
English: since, made in the likeness, they must consist of other elements, those of others, so that you never dare to stop.
MATCH

983-984
Latin: sequar (fut. 1st sg.); quodcumque dices (fut.) loqui ridere sapere; ut sit ex aliis eadem facientibus.
English: I shall press you; whatever you say can speak, laugh, be wise must be made of others doing the same.
MATCH (the added "can" is slight).

985-990
Latin: [cond.] si cernimus haec esse delira furiosaque, et (aliquis) potest ridere non ex ridentibus auctus, sapere, rationem reddere doctis dictis non ex seminibus sapientibus; [question] qui minus queant ea quae videmus sentire esse permixta seminibus carentibus sensu.
English: if we see this is raving; a man can laugh though not made of laughing things, be wise, give an account in learned words; why should things we see have sense be less able to be of seeds lacking sense.
MATCH

1002-1006

1007-1012

English: so that you may know it matters ...; and may not think that what we see floating, born, perishing can reside in eternal first bodies.

1023-1025

1026-1029

1030-1037
Latin: principio caeli colorem, quae cohibet, sidera, lunam, nitorem (hanging accusatives); si essent/si sint obiecta repente; quid magis mirabile poterat dici, aut quod minus auderent gentes credere fore; nil; ita species miranda fuisset.
English: consider the color, the stars, moon, sun; if they were thrown before mortals suddenly; what could be called more wonderful, or what would the nations less have dared to believe; nothing; so wondrous would this appearance have been.

1070-1076
Latin: si tanta copia seminum quantam aetas non queat enumerare, (si) quis (= quibus) eadem natura manet quae queat conicere semina simili ratione atque huc coniecta sunt; necesse est confiteare esse alios orbis terrarum, gentis, saecla.

1093-1104

FINDINGS
1084	LEXICAL	MINOR	0.55	"simili ratione" means on a like principle (register: "on (by) a like principle"), but the English has "on the same principle", which overstates the analogy; "Therefore you must confess, on a like principle, that sky"
975	LEXICAL	MINOR	0.55	"propritim" means "in particular, specially" (those beginnings that make up the human race in particular), but the English "properly made" reads as "correctly made"; "what of those from which the human race in particular is made?"
973	ADDITION	MINOR	0.50	"animalia quaeque" means each living creature, but the English "each kind of living creature" adds "kind" and shifts the claim from individual animals to species; "Finally, so that every living creature can feel,"
