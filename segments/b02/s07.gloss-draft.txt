973-990
Latin: [si] sensus (subj.) est tribuendus (pres. pass. periphr.) principiis eorum [animalium], uti animalia quaeque possint (subj., purpose) sentire; quid [de illis] de quibus genus humanum propritim auctumst (perf. pass.)? [primordia, implied subj., neut. pl.] scilicet cachinnant (ind.), concussa (with primordia) risu tremulo; spargunt ora genasque lacrimis; callent dicere multa de mixtura; quaerunt quae sint (subj.) sibi primordia; quando quidem [ea] adsimulata (with primordia) mortalibus debent constare ex aliis elementis, inde alia ex aliis, ut [tu] nusquam ausis (subj.) consistere; quippe sequar (fut. 1 sg.): quodcumque dices loqui ridere sapere, ut sit ex aliis facientibus eadem. quod si cernimus haec esse delira, et [quis] potest ridere non auctus ex ridentibus, et sapere et reddere rationem doctis dictis non ex seminibus sapientibus, qui minus queant (subj.) ea quae videmus sentire esse permixta seminibus carentibus sensu?
English: if sense must be granted to their beginnings so that each creature can feel, what of those from which the human race is made? They must shake with laughter, sprinkle faces, be skilled at speaking, ask what their first-beginnings are; since made like mortals they must consist of other elements, others of others, so that you never dare stop; I shall press you: whatever you say speaks, laughs, is wise is made of others doing the same. But if this is madness, and a man can laugh not made of laughing things, be wise and give account not from wise seeds, why should things we see feel be less able to be of senseless seeds?
MATCH (scilicet + indicative given as "must", within the reductio; "propritim" as "properly" is a loose reading of "in particular", kept below threshold)

English: what came from the earth goes back into the earth; what was sent from the shores of the ether is carried back and received by the precincts of heaven.

1002-1012
Latin: nec mors interemit res sic ut conficiat corpora materiai, sed dissupat coetum ollis; inde coniungit aliud aliis et efficit ut omnes res convertant formas, mutent colores, capiant sensus, reddant puncto tempore; ut noscas referre cum quibus et quali positura primordia contineantur et quos motus dent accipiantque, neve putes quod videmus fluitare in summis rebus, nasci, perire, posse residere penes aeterna corpora prima.
English: death does not annihilate the bodies of matter but scatters their union; joins one to another, brings it about that all things change shapes and colors, take on sense and give it up; so that you may know it matters with what and in what position they are held together and what motions they give and receive, and may not think what we see floating on surfaces, being born and perishing, can reside in eternal first bodies.
MATCH
