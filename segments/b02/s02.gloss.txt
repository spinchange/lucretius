**294-295.** Latin: copia materiai (subj.) fuit (perf. ind.) nec stipata magis umquam nec maioribus intervallis (neg.). English: store of matter was never more tightly packed, nor spread apart by greater intervals. MATCH ("than now" is implicit).

**296.** Latin: nam neque adaugescit quicquam neque deperit inde (pres. ind., double neg.). English: nothing is added, nothing perishes. MATCH

**297-302.** Latin:
- quo in motu corpora principiorum nunc sunt, in eodem fuere (perf.) et ferentur (fut. pass.) simili ratione.
- et quae consuerint gigni gignentur (fut. pass.) eadem condicione et erunt, crescent, valebunt, quantum cuique datum est per foedera naturai.

English: motion they are in now is the same they were in, they will be carried in like manner; what was wont to be born will be born, be, grow, be strong, as much as granted to each by nature's covenants. MATCH

**303-307.** Latin:
- nec ulla vis potest commutare rerum summam.
- nam neque est quicquam extra quo genus ullum materiai possit effugere ex omni.
- neque unde nova vis coorta queat inrumpere in omne et mutare naturam et vertere motus.

English: no force can change the sum; nothing outside into which matter could flee; no source from which a new force could burst in, change nature, turn its motions. MATCH

**308-311.** Latin:
- illud non est mirabile, quare summa videatur (pres. subj.) stare summa quiete,
- cum omnia primordia sint in motu (concessive),
- praeter quam siquid dat motus proprio corpore.

English: no wonder why, although all first-beginnings move, the sum seems to stand in utmost rest, except where something makes motions with its own body. MATCH

**312-316.** Latin:
- omnis primorum natura iacet infra sensibus.
- qua propter, ubi ipsa nequeas cernere, motus quoque debent surpere.
- praesertim cum quae possimus cernere celent motus spatio diducta.

English: the nature of the first bodies lies below the senses; since you cannot see them, their motions must steal away; especially since visible things hide their motions when withdrawn by distance. MATCH

**317-322.** Latin:

English: flocks creep on where the grasses, calling, invite each one; lambs play and butt; all seem blurred from afar, like whiteness standing on the hill. MATCH

**323-332.** Latin:

**333-337.** Latin:

**338-341.** Latin:

**342-348.** Latin: genus humanum, natantes pecudes, armenta, ferae, volucres (those that concelebrant loca aquarum, and those that pervolgant nemora): perge sumere unum quidvis generatim; invenies differre figuris. English: same list, same relative clauses, imperative, future. MATCH

FINDINGS
383	MISTRANSLATION	MODERATE	0.60	In "quare fulmineus multo penetralior ignis / quam noster fluat e taedis terrestribus ortus" the subject of fluat is the thunderbolt's fire (it "flows far more piercing"), and terrestribus modifies taedis; the English gives "flows" to our fire ("which flows from torches") and detaches "of the earth" from the torches. Suggested fix, with 382 "why the fire of the thunderbolt flows far more piercing": "than ours, which springs from torches of the earth;"
