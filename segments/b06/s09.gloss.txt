ANALYSIS
1138-1140: L: haec ratio morborum et mortifer aestus (subj) reddidit (perf. act. sg.) agros funestos; vastavit vias; exhausit urbem civibus. E: a pattern of disease like this and a heat made fields deadly; laid waste; drained the city. MATCH.
1141-1143: L: (it) veniens penitus, ortus Aegypti finibus, permensus aera multum camposque natantis, incubuit populo Pandionis omni. E: same participles; fell upon the whole people. MATCH.
1144: L: inde catervatim dabantur (impf. pass. pl.) morbo mortique. E: same. MATCH.
1145-1146: L: gerebant caput incensum fervore et oculos rubentes suffusa luce. E: same. MATCH.
1147-1150: L: fauces atrae sudabant sanguine; via vocis saepta ulceribus coibat; lingua (interpres animi) manabat cruore, debilitata, gravis, aspera. E: same. MATCH.
1151-1153: L: ubi morbida vis complerat pectus et confluxerat in cor ipsum maestum aegris; tum claustra lababant. ipsum is neuter and goes with cor. E: "the very force of the disease" flowed into the grieving heart. DIFF: ipsum moved from cor to vis.
1154-1155: L: spiritus volvebat odorem taetrum foras; ritu quo perolent cadavera proiecta. E: same. MATCH.
1156-1157: L: vires animi totius, omne corpus languebat in limine leti. E: same. MATCH.
1158-1162: L: angor erat comes malis; querella commixta gemitu; singultus coactans nervos et membra corripere dissoluebat eos, fatigans, defessos ante. E: same. MATCH.
1163-1167: L: nec posses tueri summam partem fervescere ardore nimio; sed potius (posses tueri it) proponere tactum tepidum, et omne corpus rubere ulceribus quasi inustis, ut cum sacer ignis diditur. E: "it offered"; the body reddened. MATCH (the compression keeps the sense).
1168-1169: L: pars intima flagrabat ad ossa; flamma flagrabat stomacho. E: same. MATCH.
1170-1171: L: nil leve tenveque posses vertere in utilitatem membris; at ventum et frigora semper. E: same. MATCH.
1172-1173: L: partim dabant membra in fluvios, iacientes corpus nudum. E: same. MATCH.
1174-1177: L: multi inciderunt praecipites nymphis putealibus, ore patente; sitis mersans corpora aequabat multum imbrem parvis umoribus. E: same. MATCH.
1178-1181: L: nec requies erat; corpora iacebant defessa; medicina mussabat, quippe cum versarent lumina patentia, ardentia, expertia somno. E: same. MATCH.
1182-1192: L: multa signa dabantur: mens perturbata… sputa tenvia, minuta, contacta colore croci, salsaque, edita tussi; nervi trahere, artus tremere, frigus succedere non dubitabat. salsa is an adjective on sputa ("salty"). E: "tinged with the color of saffron / and salt". DIFF: salt becomes part of the colour.
1192-1195: L: ad supremum tempus nares conpressae, acumen tenve, oculi cavati, tempora cava, pellis frigida duraque in ore, iacens rictu, frons tenta manebat. E: same. MATCH.
1196: L: nec nimio post artus iacebant rigida morte. E: same. MATCH.
1197-1198: L: octavo lumine aut nona lampade reddebant vitam. E: same. MATCH.
1199-1204: L: siquis vitarat funera, tabes letumque manebat hunc posterius ulceribus et proluvie alvi; aut sanguis corruptus ibat naribus; huc vires corpusque fluebat. E: same. MATCH.
1205-1207: L: qui exierat profluvium acre sanguinis, tamen morbus ibat huic in nervos, artus, partis genitalis. E: same. MATCH.
1208-1212: L: partim metuentes vivebant privati parte virili; non nulli manebant in vita sine manibus pedibusque; partim perdebant lumina; usque adeo metus incesserat. E: same. MATCH.
1213-1214: L: oblivia rerum cunctarum cepere quosdam; neque possent cognoscere se. E: same. MATCH.
1215-1218: L: cum corpora iacerent inhumata, tamen genus alituum ferarumque aut absiliebat ut exiret odorem, aut ubi gustarat languebat. E: same. MATCH.
1219-1222: L: nec tamen comparebat ulla avis temere; nec saecla ferarum exibant; pleraque languebant et moriebantur. E: same. MATCH.
1222-1224: L: vis canum strata viis ponebat animam aegre; vis morbida extorquebat vitam membris. E: same. MATCH.
1225-1229: L: funera certabant rapi; nec ratio remedii dabatur; nam quod aliis dederat licere volvere auras et tueri templa caeli, hoc aliis erat exitio. E: same. MATCH.
1230-1234: L: illud unum erat miserandum, quod ubi quisque videbat se implicitum morbo, ut damnatus, iacebat deficiens animo, respectans funera amittebat animam. E: same. MATCH.
1235-1238: L: nullo tempore cessabant apisci contagia ex aliis alios, tam quam pecudes; id cumulabat funere funus. E: same. MATCH.
1239-1242: L: quicumque fugitabant visere suos aegros, incuria poenibat (eos) cupidos, timentis, morte turpi, mactans desertos. E: same (the anacoluthon is kept). MATCH.
1243-1245: L: qui fuerant praesto ibant contagibus atque labore, quem pudor cogebat obire blandaque vox lassorum mixta voce querellae. pudor and vox are co-subjects of cogebat. E: they went down by contagion, by the labor that shame compelled, "and the coaxing voice". The voice reads as a third cause of death. DIFF: the co-subject is detached from cogebat.
1246: L: optimus quisque subibat hoc genus leti. E: same. MATCH.
1247-1250: L: pastor, armentarius, moderator aratri languebat; corpora contrusa casa iacebant dedita morti paupertate et morbo. E: same. MATCH.
1251-1253: L: posses videre corpora parentum super pueris, retroque natos edere vitam super matribus et patribus. E: same. MATCH.
1254-1256: L: nec minimam partem maeror confluxit ex agris in urbem, quem copia agricolarum contulit. E: same. MATCH.
1257-1258: L: conplebant loca tectaque; quo magis aestu mors accumulabat confertos acervatim. E: same. MATCH.
1259-1261: L: multa corpora prostrata siti, voluta, iacebant strata ad silanos, interclusa anima dulcedine. E: same. MATCH.
1262-1266: L: videres membra languida cum corpore semanimo, horrida, cooperta, perire, pelli una super ossibus, sepulta ulceribus sordeque. E: same. MATCH.
1267-1270: L: mors replerat delubra corporibus; templa manebant onerata cadaveribus, loca quae aedituentes complerant hospitibus. E: same. MATCH.
1271-1272: L: nec religio nec numina magni pendebantur; praesens dolor exsuperabat. E: same. MATCH.
1273-1276: L: nec mos remanebat quo populus consuerat humari; totus trepidabat; unusquisque humabat suum cognatum pro re. E: same. MATCH.
1277-1284: L: res subita et paupertas suasit multa; locabant consanguineos insuper aliena extructa rogorum, subdebant faces, rixantes, potius quam desererentur, certantes alium populum sepelire; redibant lassi; dabantur in lectum. E: same. MATCH.
1285-1286: L: nec poterat quisquam reperiri quem neque morbus nec mors nec luctus temptaret. E: same. MATCH.

FINDINGS
1189	GRAMMAR	MODERATE	0.70	"salsaque" is an adjective on sputa (the spittle was salty), but the English runs on from 1188, "tinged with the color of saffron / and salt", so salt becomes a second colour; fix: "and salty, brought up through the throat with difficulty by a hoarse cough."
1245	GRAMMAR	MODERATE	0.62	"blandaque lassorum vox… querellae" is the second subject of cogebat (1244) alongside pudor (shame and the weary's coaxing voice compelled them), but the English makes it a third thing by which they "went down" (1243); fix, with 1244 ending "the labor they were then compelled to undergo by": "shame, and the coaxing voice of the weary mixed with the voice of complaint."
1151	GRAMMAR	MINOR	0.50	"ipsum" is neuter and goes with "cor" in 1152 ("into the very heart"), but the English attaches it to the force of the disease ("the very / force"); fix: "Then, when through the throat it had filled the chest, and into the very"
