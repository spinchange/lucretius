246-248: L: putandumst [impers. gerundive] + fulmina gignier (pres. pass. inf.) e nubibus crassis alte extructis; nam nulla (fulmina) mittuntur (pres. pass. pl.) caelo sereno nec nubibus leviter densis umquam. E: we must suppose thunderbolts are born from thick clouds heaped high; for none are ever sent from clear sky nor from slightly dense clouds. MATCH
249-255: L: res manifesta docet (pres.) hoc fieri dubio procul; quod nubes concrescunt per totum aera, ut reamur (subj., result) omnis tenebras liquisse Acherunta et complesse cavernas caeli; usque adeo, nocte nimborum coorta (abl. abs.), ora formidinis inpendent superne, cum tempestas coeptat commoliri fulmina. E: plain fact shows this; clouds grow dense through whole air, so that we would think all darkness left Acheron and filled the sky's caverns; so far, night of storm-clouds risen, faces of terror hang above, when tempest begins to work up bolts. FINDINGS
6.296	LEXICAL	MODERATE	0.60	"in validam maturo culmine nubem" (a strong cloud whose crest/summit is ripe) is rendered "when its thunderbolt is ripe", which reads the variant fulmine, not the culmine printed in the source text (if the variant is deliberate, it needs a note); suggested fix: "falls upon a strong cloud whose crest is ripe;"
6.319	OMISSION	MINOR	0.55	"nec temere ... esse potest" ("it can hardly / not readily be") loses temere, so Lucretius's hedged "it can scarcely be wholly cold" becomes the absolute "Nor can ... be wholly and utterly cold"; suggested fix: "Nor can the force of the wind readily be wholly and utterly cold,"
