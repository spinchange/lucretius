ANALYSIS
470-472: Latin: [corpora] vestes suspensae (nom.) declarant (pres. ind. 3pl) naturam tollere (inf.) permulta corpora mari toto quoque; cum concipiunt (3pl, subj. vestes) umoris adhaesum; litore with suspensae. English: clothes declare that nature lifts very many bodies from the whole sea; when they are hung on the shore and take in a clinging of moisture. MATCH.
473-474: Latin: quo magis multa videntur (pass. 3pl) posse consurgere e salso momine ponti ad nubis augendas (purpose). English: so it seems all the more that many bodies can rise from the salt surge to swell the clouds. MATCH.
475: Latin: nam ratio omnis umoribus consanguineast. English: for the principle of all moistures is of one blood. MATCH.
476-482: Latin: videmus nebulas aestumque surgere ex fluviis et de terra; quae (rel. → nebulas aestumque) expressa feruntur, suffundunt caelum, sufficiunt nubis conveniundo; urget enim aetheris aestus et subtexit caerula nimbis. English: we see mists and heat rise; which are pressed out and carried, suffuse the sky, furnish clouds by coming together; for the ether's heat presses and weaves rain-cloud beneath the blue. MATCH.
483-484: Latin: fit ut veniant (subj.) in hunc caelum extrinsecus illa corpora quae faciunt nubis nimbosque. English: same. MATCH.
485-488: Latin: docui numerum innumerabilem summamque profundi esse infinitam; ostendi quanta mobilitate corpora volarent, quam repente transire solerent per spatium. English: I have taught the number is past counting and the sum infinite; I have shown with what great *speed* bodies fly and how in a moment they pass across a space. DIFF: mobilitate rendered "speed", against the register.
489-494: Latin: haut mirumst si tempestas atque tenebrae coperiant (subj.) maria ac terras, tam magnis ventis, inpensa superne; quandoquidem exitus introitusque redditus extat elementis per caulas aetheris et spiracula mundi. English: same. MATCH.
495-502: Latin: expediam (fut.) quo pacto umor concrescat et decidat; vincam (fut.) semina aquai consurgere simul nubibus ipsis ex omnibus rebus, et utrumque (nubis et aquam) crescere pariter, ut nobis corpus cum sanguine crescit, sudor, umor. English: same. MATCH.
503-505: Latin: [nubes] concipiunt saepe multum marinum umorem, uti vellera, cum venti nubila portant supera mare. English: same. MATCH.
506-507a: Latin: consimili ratione umor tollitur (pass.) ex omnibus amnibus in nubis. English: *In like manner* moisture is lifted from all rivers into the clouds. DIFF: consimili ratione not given the register's rendering.
507b-512: Latin: cum semina aquarum convenere adaucta, confertae nubes certant mittere umorem dupliciter; nam vis venti contrudit et copia nimborum coacta urget, premit, facit effluere imbris. English: same. MATCH.
513-516: Latin: cum nubila rarescunt ventis aut dissolvuntur icta calore, mittunt umorem stillantque, quasi cera multa liquescat (subj.) tabescens super igni. English: same. MATCH.
517-518: Latin: imber vehemens fit ubi nubila premuntur utraque, vi cumulata et impete venti. English: same. MATCH.
519-523: Latin: pluviae consuerunt retinere diu morarique ubi semina cientur, nubes nimbique feruntur, terraque redhalat umorem. English: same. MATCH.
524-526: Latin: ubi sol fulsit (perf.) adversa aspargine nimborum, tum color arqui existit in nigris nubibus. English: same. MATCH.
527-534: Latin: cetera (nix, venti, grando, pruinae, vis geli, mora) perfacilest reperire animoque videre quo pacto fiant, cum cognoris quae sint reddita elementis. English: same. MATCH.
535-536a: Latin: percipe (imper.) quae ratio extet (subj.) terrai motibus. English: learn by what principle the motions of the earth come about. DIFF: register fixes 6.535 as "what the principle is"; the English makes the motions, not the principle, the thing that "comes about".
536b-542: Latin: fac ut rearis terram esse plenam speluncis, gerere lacus, lucunas, rupes; putandumst flumina tecta volvere fluctus summersos; res postulat similem esse sui. English: same. MATCH.
543-547: Latin: his rebus subiunctis (abl. abs.), terra tremit concussa ruinis, ubi aetas subruit speluncas; cadunt montes, tremores disserpunt. English: same. MATCH.
548-551: Latin: tecta tota tremescunt concussa plaustris non magno pondere; exultant si quidvis viai succutit orbes. English: same. MATCH.
552-556: Latin: fit ut terra iactetur vacillans ubi gleba provolvitur in lucunas; ut vas non quit constare nisi umor destitit iactarier. English: same. MATCH.
557-560: Latin: cum ventus collectus procumbit et urget speluncas, tellus incumbit quo vis prona premit. English: same. MATCH.
561-564: Latin: quae extructa sunt domorum, quanto edita magis, minent inclinata; trabes inpendent ire paratae. English: same. MATCH.
565-567: Latin: et metuunt credere exitiale tempus clademque manere naturam mundi, cum videant molem incumbere. English: and yet men fear to believe that a fatal time waits for the world's nature, even when they see the mass leaning. MATCH.
568-574: Latin: quod nisi respirent venti, vis nulla refrenet res euntis; nunc quia respirant, ingravescunt, redeunt, cedunt, terra minitatur ruinas saepius quam facit; inclinatur, recellit, recipit sedes. English: same. MATCH.

585-587: Latin: quod accidit Sidone et fuit Aegi, quas urbes exitus animai et terrae motus disturbat. English: same. MATCH.

591-595: Latin: quod nisi prorumpit, impetus animai et vis venti dispertitur ut horror et incutit tremorem; frigus uti cum venit in artus, concutit invitos cogens tremere. English: same. MATCH.
596-600: Latin: trepidant ancipiti terrore; tecta timent, metuunt ne natura dissoluat cavernas, neu distracta dispandat hiatum idque velit complere ruinis. English: passive "be undone by nature", same agency. MATCH.
601-607: Latin: licet reantur caelum terramque fore incorrupta; tamen vis pericli subdit stimulum timoris, ne tellus subtracta feratur in barathrum, summa sequatur, fiat ruina. English: same. MATCH.
