879-882: Lat: fons est frigidus (main); rel. supra quem stuppa (subj) sita iacit flammam (pres. ind.), abl. abs. concepto protinus igni; taeda (subj) accensa consimili ratione conlucet (pres. ind.) per undas; rel. quocumque natans (taeda) impellitur (pres. pass.) auris. Eng: spring is cold; tow, set above it, throws flame, catching fire at once; torch, lit on a like principle, shines on the waves wherever, floating, it is driven by breezes. MATCH
883-887: Lat: nimirum quia sunt semina vaporis permulta in aqua; necessest ignis corpora (acc. subj.) consurgere de terra per fontem, exspirare foras, exire in auras; non ita multa tamen ut fons calidus queat fieri (result). Eng: because there are many seeds of heat in the water; bodies of fire must rise from the earth through the spring, breathe out, go forth into the breezes; yet not so many that the spring could become hot. 888-889: Lat: vis (subj) cogit (pres. ind.) dispersa (semina) erumpere foras per aquam subito et sursum conciliari. Eng: a force compels them, scattered, to break out suddenly through the water and gather above. MATCH
890-894: Lat: quod genus est fons Aradi in mari, qui dulcis aquai scatit et dimovet salsas undas circum se; aequor (subj) praebet utilitatem sitientibus nautis multis regionibus, quod dulcis (undas) intervomit inter salsas. Eng: like the spring at Aradus in the sea, which gushes fresh water and pushes away the salt waves; the sea offers a timely service to thirsty sailors in many regions, in that it spews up fresh water among the salt. MATCH
895-899: Lat: illa (semina) possunt erumpere per fontem et scatere foras; quae semina cum conveniunt in stuppam aut adhaerent in corpore taedai, ardescunt facile extemplo, quia stuppae taedaeque (subj) tenentes habent in se multa semina ignis. Eng: those seeds can break out and bubble forth; when they come together in the tow or cling to the torch, they blaze at once, because tow and torches also hold many seeds of fire. MATCH

FINDINGS
1000	LEXICAL	MODERATE	0.65	"ratio reddetur" (future passive, the register's rationem reddere = "give a reasoning", listed for 6.1000) becomes "the reasoning comes easily", losing both "given" and the future, and leaving "patefiet" in 1001 without its future verb; "for the rest, from here a reasoning will easily be given, and the whole" (with 1001 "cause will be laid open, that lures the force of iron.")
956	GRAMMAR	MODERATE	0.50	"tempestate ... coorta" is a singular ablative absolute ("when a storm has arisen") in the printed text, but the English makes it a plural nominative subject ("storms that have arisen ... withdraw"), not the text "as Bailey's text stands" per the note; "and when a storm has risen up in earth and sky," (with 957 "they withdraw, far off, into sky and earth, as is right;")
