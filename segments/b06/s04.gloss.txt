ANALYSIS

**470-472**
- Latin: vestes suspensae litore (subj.) declarant (pres. ind. pl.) + acc.-inf. naturam tollere permulta corpora mari toto; temporal cum concipiunt (pres. ind., subj. vestes) umoris adhaesum.
- English: clothes declare that nature lifts very many bodies from the whole sea, when they, hung on the shore, take in moisture.
- MATCH

**473-475**
- Latin: quo magis videntur (pres. pass., subj. multa) posse consurgere e salso momine ponti, ad nubes augendas (gerundive, purpose); nam ratio omnis (nom., with ratio) consanguinea est umoribus (dat.).
- English: so it seems the more that many bodies can rise from the salt surge, to swell the clouds; for the principle of all moistures is of one blood.
- MATCH: omnis is shifted from ratio to umoribus, but the kinship claim survives.

**476-482**
- Latin: videmus (1 pl.) nebulas aestumque surgere de fluviis omnibus et de terra ipsa; quae (rel., the mists and heat) expressa feruntur sursum ut halitus, suffundunt caelum caligine, sufficiunt nubes altas conveniundo; enim urget (subj. aestus aetheris signiferi) super, et subtexit (same subj.) caerula nimbis quasi densendo.
- English: same subjects and verbs; the ether's heat presses from above and weaves rain-cloud beneath the blue.
- MATCH

**483-484**
- Latin: fit ut veniant (subj. illa corpora quae faciunt nubes nimbosque volantes) in hunc caelum extrinsecus.
- English: the same.
- MATCH

**485-488**

- English: the same.
- MATCH

**489-494**
- Latin: haud mirum est si coperiant (pres. subj., subj. tempestas atque tenebrae) maria ac terras parvo tempore saepe, tam magnis ventis (abl.), impensa superne; quandoquidem extat exitus introitusque redditus elementis (dat.) per caulas aetheris omnis et per spiracula mundi.
- English: no wonder if storm and darkness, with such winds, cover seas and lands, looming above, since a way out and in stands granted to the elements through the pores and breathing-holes.
- MATCH

**495-497a**

**497b-502**

**513-516**

**517-518**
