**1002-1004**

- English: seeds or current must flow; which drives apart all the air; that is lodged anywhere between.
- MATCH ("all… anywhere" renders -cumque).

**1005-1008**

**1098-1102**

FINDINGS
1101	AMBIGUITY	MODERATE	0.60	In "ubi putorem umida nactast… icta" the subject is the earth (umida, icta feminine with terra), but the English "when, being damp, it has gathered rot" most naturally takes "it" as the rising force of disease, so the rot and the soaking by rains and suns attach to the wrong noun; "surges up from the earth, when the earth, being damp, has gathered rot,"
1131	LEXICAL	MINOR	0.55	"consimili ratione" is fixed in the register as "on a like principle", with 6.1131 named, but the English has "In a like way"; "On a like principle pestilence often comes upon cattle too,"
1121	MISTRANSLATION	MODERATE	0.50	In "ut nebula ac nubes paulatim repit et omne / qua graditur conturbat et immutare coactat" the air (aer inimicus, 1120) is the subject that creeps, confounds and forces change, with mist and cloud only the comparison, but the English makes mist and cloud the subjects of crawl, throw into confusion and force (1121-1122); "it creeps little by little like mist and cloud, and throws into confusion"
