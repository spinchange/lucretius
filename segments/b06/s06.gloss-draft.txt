760-766
Latin: omnia (quae) naturali ratione geruntur [pres. pass.]; et apparet origo, e quibus causis fiant.
Purpose clauses, both governed by ne:
- ne credatur [subj. pass.] ianua Orci pote esse eis regionibus;
- [ne] post hinc reamur [subj., 1 pl.] deos manis forte inferne ducere animas in oras Acheruntis.
Comparison: ut cervi alipedes saepe putantur naribus ducere serpentia saecla de latebris.
English: all these things are brought about by a natural principle; their causes and origin are plain; "so that it may not be believed there is a gateway of Orcus..., and we then suppose that spirits are led ... by the gods of the shades"; as stags are often thought to draw out the crawling generations.
DIFF: the negation of ne does not clearly carry over to reamur, so "and we then suppose" can read as an affirmative result; pote (can be) is flattened to "there is".

767-768
Latin: percipe [imperative]; quam procul sit [subj.] repulsum a vera ratione; nam conor [pres.] nunc dicere de re ipsa.
English: take note how far this is driven from true reasoning; for now I attempt to speak of the thing itself.
MATCH

769-772
Latin: principio dico hoc (quod dixi saepe quoque ante): esse in terra figuras rerum cuiusque modi; multa [esse] vitalia, quae sunt cibo; multaque, quae possint [subj.] incutere morbos et adcelerare mortem.
Subject of incutere: multa (quae). Object: morbos.
English: I say this, as often before; that in the earth there are shapes of every kind; many are life-giving as food; and "many that diseases / can drive in upon us and that can hasten death".
DIFF: word order makes "diseases" read as the subject of "can drive in", with the things as object; the Latin has the things inflicting the diseases.

FINDINGS
771	AMBIGUITY	MAJOR	0.65	In "multaque, morbos / incutere ... quae possint" (771-772) the things inflict the diseases, but the English "many that diseases / can drive in upon us" makes "diseases" read as the subject, with the things driven in. Fix: "many that are life-giving, serving as food, and many that can drive diseases"
851	MISTRANSLATION	MODERATE	0.55	"putant partim" (850-851) means that some of the men think the spring is heated by the sun beneath the earth, but the English "it partly grows hot" makes partim qualify the heating. Fix: "sun beneath the earth, some suppose, it grows hot"
762	AMBIGUITY	MODERATE	0.50	The single ne governs both "credatur" (763) and "reamur" (764), but "so that it may not be believed ..., / and we then suppose" lets the second clause read as an affirmed consequence; "pote ... esse" (can be) is also flattened to "there is". Fix: "lest it be believed there can be a gateway of Orcus in these regions," (with 763 "or we then suppose that spirits are led from here to the shores of Acheron,")
