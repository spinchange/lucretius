[DRAFT] (4.144) The lacuna follows 144 and 145 resumes mid-argument: the lost lines must have introduced the surface-film account (the "summum quicquid", 145), and "semper enim" gives the reason for a claim now missing. The translation prints [lacuna] unnumbered and supplies nothing. Note also that 143-144 is a program ("Nunc ea… genantur") whose main verb is lost, so the English sentence is deliberately left hanging.

[DRAFT] (4.145) "Summum… de rebus" is the "outermost" film that things continually shed. This is the doctrine of the simulacra (here "images"), thin skins of atoms that keep the shape of their source. Epicurean doctrine holds that seeing occurs when these strike the eye.

[DRAFT] (4.150) The mirror argument explains reflection: the images cannot pass through a dense, smooth surface ("splendida… densaque… levor") and are not torn by it, so they rebound ("redundent", 154). Compare cloth (porous, passes them) and rock or wood (rough, shreds them).

[DRAFT] (4.163) "Ratione" (also 143, 191, 254) is rendered "principle" where it means the manner or mechanism of a process, in keeping with the register's "by what principle." It is not the doctrine "reasoning."

[DRAFT] (4.170) Acherunta ("Acheron"): the underworld river stands for the underworld itself, so the darkness of the storm is imagined as rising from Hades. Lucretius uses mythological imagery even as he denies its reality.

[DRAFT] (4.173) "Formidinis ora," the "faces of Terror," personifies fear as a figure with many faces, as the storm clouds seem to be. Compare the cloud-shapes of 4.136-140. The image echoes the "terror of the mind" that the proem says must be dispelled (1.146-148).

[DRAFT] (4.180) Lines 180-182 recur verbatim at 4.909-911, where they introduce the discussion of sleep. The English repeats them there word for word. The swan and the cranes belong to the Hellenistic tradition of the poet's modest "sweet" verse set against loud verbosity.

[DRAFT] (4.186) "E primis… minutis" renders "primis" as "first bodies" in keeping with the register's corpora prima, although the Latin has only the shorthand "primis." The "minute first bodies" are what make light and heat swift.

[DRAFT] (4.190) "Protelo": a technical term from ox-driving for a team yoked in tandem, each animal pulling the next in line; "stimulatur fulgere fulgur" ("flash goaded by flash") carries the image of one beam driving the next. The translation keeps the sense with "a team in tandem."

[DRAFT] (4.198) "Aeris intervallum": the manuscripts show "aeris," which the Perseus text normalizes as "aëris" in two places (187, 198). The translation reads the same word and gives "interval of air" both times, as a refrain.

[DRAFT] (4.206) "Quone… debere" is a rhetorical argument from the greater to the lesser: if the sun's light, which comes from deep inside, crosses the sky in a moment, images shed from the very surface must be faster. It argues from the faster to the slower.

[DRAFT] (4.211) The reflection of the stars in water is offered as proof ("specimen verum", 209) of the images' speed, since the stars appear in the water the instant it is set out. "Mundi" in 213 is rendered "of the world," so "stars of the world" means the stars of the whole cosmos.

[DRAFT] (4.220) "Exesor moerorum" ("eater of walls") describes the sea's spray as eroding walls along the shore, which shows that even such salty effluences are bodies leaving the sea. Lucretius uses it to argue that every kind of sensation depends on material streams.

[DRAFT] (4.224) "Absinthia" is wormwood, the bitter herb. Lucretius uses it again at 1.936-950, the famous honey-on-the-cup simile that returns at 4.11-25; the bitter taste here is a sensory example of effluence.

[DRAFT] (4.229) "Sentire sonare": "sense them sounding." Hearing, smell and sight are all treated as the reception of material streams, as the preceding examples have argued. The verse states compressed Epicurean doctrine that all perception is contact with bodies.

[DRAFT] (4.236) "Species" here is rendered "sight" in "fall upon our sight." The register gives "species" as "face, appearance, likeness"; the context of vision decides. The square shape felt in the dark is supposed to be known in the light only through its image.

[DRAFT] (4.246) The air between object and eye is pushed along ahead of the image and "sweeps the pupils," so that distance is judged by the quantity of air driven before it. This is the Epicurean account of depth perception.
