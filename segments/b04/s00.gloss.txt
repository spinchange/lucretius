ANALYSIS
1-2a: Latin: (ego) peragro [pres. act. 1sg] avia loca Pieridum; loca trita [modifies loca] nullius solo ante. English: I range through pathless places, trodden by no one's foot before. MATCH
2b-5: Latin: iuvat accedere fontis integros atque haurire; iuvat decerpere flores novos, petere coronam insignem meo capiti inde, unde nulli prius velarint [perf. subj. 3pl] tempora musae. English: it is a joy to come, drink, pluck, seek a crown from where the Muses have veiled no one's temples before. MATCH
6-9: Latin: primum quod doceo de magnis rebus et pergo exsolvere animum nodis artis religionum; deinde quod pango carmina tam lucida de re obscura, contingens [ego] cuncta lepore musaeo. English: first because I teach and go on to free the mind; then because I compose lucid verses, touching all with charm. MATCH
10: Latin: id quoque videtur non ab nulla ratione (enim). English: for this too seems not without good reason. MATCH
11-25: Latin: nam uti medentes, cum conantur dare pueris absinthia taetra, prius contingunt oras circum pocula liquore mellis, ut aetas puerorum inprovida ludificetur [pres. subj. pass.] tenus labrorum, interea perpotet laticem amarum, decepta non capiatur, sed recreata valescat; sic ego, quoniam haec ratio videtur tristior quibus non est tractata, volgus abhorret, volui exponere rationem nostram carmine Pierio et contingere melle, si possem tenere animum tuum versibus, dum percipis naturam rerum ac persentis utilitatem. English: physicians touch the rims with honey so that children's age may be fooled, drink, not be undone, grow strong; so I, since this reasoning seems grim to those who have not handled it and the crowd shrinks, have wished to set out our reasoning and touch it with honey, if I might hold your mind while you perceive nature and feel its usefulness. MATCH
26-32: Latin: quoniam docui qualia sint exordia, quam variis distantia formis volitent sponte sua percita motu aeterno, quo modo possit res quaeque creari ex his; nunc incipiam agere quod attinet: esse ea quae vocamus simulacra rerum, quae nominitandast quasi membranae vel cortex. English: since I have taught what the origins are like, how, differing in shapes, they fly of their own accord driven by motion, how each thing can be created; I shall begin to treat that there exist images, to be named films or bark. MATCH
33-39: Latin: quoniam docui quid esset natura animi, quibus e rebus compta cum corpore vigeret, quo modo distracta rediret in ordia prima; nunc incipiam agere... esse simulacra, quod imago gerit speciem ac formam similem eius, cuius de corpore cluet fusa vagari. English: since I have taught what the mind's nature is, from what things joined with the body it thrives, how torn apart it returns; I shall treat that images exist, because the image carries a likeness of the thing from whose body it is said to be shed and wander. MATCH
40-50: Latin: quae, quasi membranae dereptae de summo corpore, volitant per auras; eadem obvia nobis vigilantibus et in somnis terrificant mentes, cum contuimur figuras miras et simulacra carentum luce, quae nos languentis sopore excierunt; ne reamur animas effugere Acherunte, aut umbras volitare, neve aliquid nostri posse relinqui post mortem, cum corpus et natura animi perempta dederint discessum in primordia sua. English: which like films torn fly through the air; these same, meeting us awake, terrify minds, and in sleep when we behold shapes and images of those lacking light, which roused us languid in sleep; lest we think spirits escape Acheron, shades fly, something of us be left, when body and mind destroyed have gone apart into first-beginnings. MATCH
51-53: Latin: dico effigias et figuras tenuis mitti [pres. pass. inf.] ab rebus de summo cortice; id licet cognoscere hinc corde quamvis hebeti. English: I say effigies and thin shapes are sent off from their outermost bark; one may know this however dull. MATCH
54-62: Latin: quoniam res multae mittunt corpora, partim diffusa solute (ut robora fumum, ignes vaporem), partim contexta magis (cum cicadae ponunt tunicas, vituli nascentes mittunt membranas, serpens exuit vestem); nam videmus vepres auctas spoliis. English: since many things send off bodies, some diffused, some dense, as cicadas, calves, serpent; we see brambles decked with spoils. MATCH
63-64: Latin: quoniam quae fiunt, imago tenuis debet mitti de summo corpore. English: since these happen, a thin image must be sent off. MATCH
65-66: Latin: nulla potestas hiscendi cur illa cadant et recedant magis quam quae tenvia sunt. English: same. MATCH
67-71: Latin: praesertim cum sint corpora multa minuta in summis rebus, quae possint iaci ordine eodem et servare figuram formai, et multo citius, quanto minus queunt indupediri pauca et locata prima fronte. English: same, "being few and placed in the very front". MATCH
72-74: Latin: videmus multa [subj. acc.] iacere ac largiri [act. inf.], non solum ex alto, verum de summis ipsum colorem [obj.]. English: we see many things thrown off and lavished [pass.]... the very color too. DIFF: the things become what is thrown, not what throws off; colorem loses its verb.
75-80: Latin: vela lutea russaque et ferrugina faciunt id, cum intenta theatris flutant trementia per malos trabesque volgata; inficiunt consessum caveai et omnem speciem scaenai, patrum matrumque, coguntque fluitare colore suo. English: awnings do this when stretched they flap, spread on masts and beams; they stain the assembly and the face of the stage, fathers and mothers, and make them ripple with their color. MATCH
81-83: Latin: quanto magis moenia theatri sunt inclusa circum, tam magis haec omnia intus, perfusa lepore, conrident, luce diei correpta [abl. abs.: daylight cut off/curtailed]. English: the more the walls are shut in, the more everything inside laughs, "with the light of day drawn in". DIFF: correpta (shut out, cut short) read as light admitted.
84-86: Latin: ergo cum lintea mittunt fucum de summo corpore, res quaeque debent mittere effigias tenvis, quoniam utraque iaculantur ex summo. English: same. MATCH
87-89: Latin: sunt igitur vestigia certa formarum, quae volitant praedita filo subtili nec possunt videri singillatim secreta. English: same. MATCH
90-94: Latin: odor, fumus, vapor et aliae res ideo abundant diffusae, quia dum veniunt extrinsecus ortae ex alto, scinduntur per iter flexum, nec sunt ostia recta viarum qua contendant exire. English: same. MATCH
95-97: Latin: cum membrana tenuis coloris iacitur, nihil est quod possit discerpere eam, quoniam est in promptu locata prima fronte. English: same. MATCH
98-101: Latin: simulacra quae apparent in speculis, in aqua, in omni splendore, necessest consistere imaginibus missis eorum, quandoquidem praedita sunt specie simili rerum. English: same. MATCH
102-103: = 65-66. MATCH
104-109: Latin: sunt tenues effigiae formarum similesque illis, quas cum nemo possit cernere singillatim, reiectae repulsu reddunt visum ex aequore speculorum; nec videntur posse servari ratione alia, ut figurae tam similes reddantur cuique. English: same. MATCH
110-115: Latin: percipe quam tenui natura constet imago; quoniam primordia sunt tantum infra sensus et minora quam quae oculi coeptant non posse tueri, uti confirmem id, percipe quam subtilia sint exordia. English: same. MATCH
116-117: Latin: animalia sunt partim tantula, eorum tertia pars nulla ratione possit videri. English: same. MATCH
118-122: Latin: quale putandumst intestinum quodvis horum; quid globus cordis aut oculi, membra, artus; quantula sunt; quid primordia unde constet anima atque natura animi; nonne vides quam subtilia. English: same. MATCH
123-126: Latin: quaecumque expirant odorem acrem (panaces, absinthia, habrotonum, centaurea), quorum unum quidvis si leviter duobus... [lacuna]. English: same, broken off. MATCH
127-128: Latin: quin potius noscas simulacra vagari multa modis multis, nulla vi cassaque sensu? English: same. MATCH
129-140: Latin: ne putes ea sola vagari quae recedunt ab rebus, sunt etiam quae gignuntur sponte et constituuntur in caelo (aer), quae feruntur sublime, ut cernimus nubes concrescere et violare speciem mundi, aera mulcentes; nam ora Gigantum videntur volare et ducere umbram, montes et saxa ante ire et succedere praeter solem, belua trahere nimbos. English: same. MATCH
141-142: Latin: nec liquentia cessant mutare speciem et vertere in oras formarum cuiusque modi. English: same. MATCH
FINDINGS
4.83	LEXICAL	MODERATE	0.60	"correpta luce diei" is an ablative absolute meaning the daylight is cut off or shut out by the awnings, but the English "with the light of day drawn in" suggests daylight let into the theater; "laughs, now that the light of day is shut out."
4.72	GRAMMAR	MINOR	0.50	"iacere ac largiri multa videmus" makes multa the things that throw off and give out (active infinitives, with colorem in 4.74 as their object), but the English "we see many things thrown off and lavished" makes them what is thrown and leaves "the very color" without a verb; "For certainly we see many things throwing off and lavishing bodies,"
