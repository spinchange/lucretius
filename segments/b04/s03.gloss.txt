ANALYSIS
379. L: we (concedimus, pres. ind., neg. nec) do not grant [that the eyes (acc.) are deceived (falli, pres. inf. pass.) hilum]. E: we do not grant that the eyes are deceived one whit. MATCH
380-385. L: [to see (tueri) where light and shadow are (sit, subj.)] is theirs (illorum est); [whether the lights are the same or not (sint necne), and whether the shadow that was here now passes there (transeat), or rather what we said before happens (fiat)], this the reasoning of the mind alone (demum) must (debet) discern; nor can (possunt, neg.) the eyes know the nature of things. E: same clauses, same subjects, negation kept. MATCH
386. L: proinde, do not (noli, imper.) fasten (adfingere) this fault of the mind (animi vitium) upon the eyes (dat.). E: same. MATCH
387-388. L: the ship in which we are carried (vehimur) moves (fertur) when it seems (videtur) to stand; the one that stays (manet) at mooring is believed (creditur) to go past. E: same. MATCH
389-390. L: hills and plains seem (videntur) to flee astern; these (quos, acc.) we drive past (agimus praeter) with the ship and we fly (volamus) with sails. E: same. MATCH
391-394. L: all the stars (cuncta) seem to rest, fixed (adfixa, agrees with sidera) to the ether's vaults; and all are (sunt) in constant motion, since, once risen (exorta), they revisit (revisunt) their far settings when they have measured out (permensa sunt) the sky with their bright body. E: same. MATCH
395-396. L: the sun and moon seem (videtur) to stay at rest on a like principle; the fact itself (res ipsa) shows (indicat) that they (ea) are carried (ferri). E: same. MATCH
397-399. L: mountains (montis, acc.) stand out far off from mid-sea, between which (inter quos) a wide free passage lies open (patet) for fleets (dat.); yet one island seems (videtur) to be formed from these joined together (coniunctis ex his). E: the mountains seem to make one island, joined. MATCH
400-403. L: it happens (fit uti) so strongly that the halls seem (videantur) to children (dat.) to turn and the columns to run round, when they themselves (ipsi) have stopped (desierunt) spinning, that they can (possint) hardly believe the whole roof is not (non) threatening (minari) to fall in on them. E: same, with negation inside "believe". MATCH
404-413. L: when nature begins (coeptat) to raise the red radiance and lift it above the mountains, those mountains (quos) above which the sun seems (videtur) to you to be, touching (contingens, agrees with sol) them close with its fire, are (absunt) scarcely two thousand bowshots away, often scarcely five hundred javelin casts; between them and the sun lie (iacent) the sea's levels, spread (substrata, agrees with aequora) beneath the ether's shores, and many thousands of lands are set (interiecta sunt) between, which peoples and beasts hold (retinent). E: same. MATCH
414-419. L: a pool of water (coniectus, nom.) no deeper than one finger, which (qui) stands (sistit) between the stones, offers (praebet) a view under the earth as deep as the sky's gulf gapes (patet) above the earth, so that you seem (videare) to look down on clouds and see sky and bodies hidden (abdita, agrees with corpora) in a sky under the earth. E: same. MATCH
420-425. L: when our horse has stuck (obhaesit) in midstream and we have looked down (despeximus), a force (vis, nom.) seems (videtur) to carry (ferre) the standing horse's body (acc.) sideways and thrust (contrudere) it upstream; and wherever we have cast (traiecimus) our eyes, all things seem (videntur) to be carried and flow in the same way. E: same, vis is the subject. MATCH
426-431. L: though (quamvis) a colonnade is (est) of even line and stands propped (suffulta) on equal columns, yet when it is seen (videtur) whole from the far end, it draws (trahit) gradually into a narrow cone's point, joining (iungens, agrees with porticus) roof to floor and right to left, until (donec) it has drawn (conduxit) them into the dim tip. E: same. MATCH
432-435. L: on the sea it happens (fit uti) that to sailors (dat.) the sun, risen (ortus) from the waves, seems (videatur) to set in the waves and hide its light, since indeed (quippe ubi) they see (tuentur) nothing but water and sky; so that you do not (ne) lightly believe (credas) the senses are shaken (labefactari) on all sides. E: same. MATCH
436-437. L: to those ignorant (ignaris, dat.) of the sea, ships in harbor seem (videntur) to strain (obnitier) crippled (clauda) against the waves, sterns broken (aplustris fractis, abl. abs.). E: same. MATCH
438-442. L: whatever part of the oars is raised (edita est) above the brine (rorem salis) is straight (recta est), and the rudders are straight above; the parts that go down (obeunt) sunk (demersa) in the water seem (videntur) broken (refracta), all to turn about (converti), to bend back (reverti) upward, and to float (fluitare) near the surface. E: same, but "salt spray" for rorem salis puts the water line in the spray rather than at the sea's surface. DIFF (lexical, minor)
443-446. L: when winds carry (portant) thin clouds at night, the shining signs seem (videntur) to glide (labier) against the clouds and go above in a direction far other than that in which, in true reasoning (vera ratione), they are carried (feruntur). E: same. MATCH
447-452. L: if a hand (manus, nom.) placed (subdita) under one eye has pressed (pressit) it from below, it happens (fit uti) that all things we see seem (videantur) to become double: the lamps' lights double, the furniture doubling (geminare) through the house, men's faces double and their bodies two. E: same. MATCH
453-461. L: when sleep (somnus, nom.) has bound (devinxit) the limbs and the body lies (iacet) at rest, we seem (videmur) to wake and move our limbs; in night's darkness we think (censemus) we see the sun; in a closed place we seem to change (mutare) sky, sea, rivers, mountains and cross plains; and to hear sounds when the night's silences hold (constent, cum + subj.); and, silent (tacentes, agrees with the subject "we"), to give (reddere) replies. E: same. MATCH
462-468. L: we see (videmus) many other wonders, which (quae) all seek (quaerunt) to violate trust in the senses (sensibus, dat.), in vain, since (quoniam) most of them deceive (fallit) through the mind's suppositions, which we add (addimus) ourselves, so that (ut) things not seen are (sint) as seen; for nothing is harder than to separate (secernere) plain things from doubtful ones, which (quas, attached to dubiis) the mind adds (addit). E: same, relative on the doubtful ones. MATCH
469-470. L: if anyone thinks (putat) that nothing is known (sciri), he does not know (nescit) whether that too can (possit) be known, since he admits (fatetur) he knows nothing. E: same. MATCH
471-472. L: against him (hunc), then, I will forbear (minuam) to argue (contendere) the case, who (qui) has set (in statuit) himself with his head in his own footprints. E: same, with an interpretive gloss on the image. MATCH
473-477. L: even granting (uti concedam) that he knows (scire) this, I will ask (quaeram) this very thing: since (cum) he has seen (viderit) nothing true before, from where he knows (sciat) what knowing and not knowing are (sit), what thing created (crearit) the notion of true and false, and what proved (probarit) that the doubtful differs from the certain. E: same. MATCH
478-479. L: you will find (invenies) that the notion of the true was created (esse creatam) first from the senses, and that the senses cannot (neque posse) be refuted (refelli). E: same. MATCH
480-481. L: for that (illud) must (debet) be found (reperirier) of greater trust which (quod) can (possit) of its own accord overcome false things with true (veris, abl.). E: same. MATCH
482-485. L: what (quid) ought (debet) to be held (haberi) in greater trust than the senses? Or will reasoning (ratio), sprung (orta) from false sense, prevail (valebit) to speak against them (eos), reasoning which (quae) has sprung wholly from the senses? Unless (nisi) they (qui) are true, all reasoning becomes (fit) false. E: same. MATCH
486-488. L: will the ears (aures, nom.) be able (poterunt) to correct (reprehendere) the eyes (oculos, acc.), or touch (tactus, nom.) the ears (aures, acc.)? Will the mouth's taste (sapor, nom.) accuse (arguet) this touch (tactum, acc.); will the nostrils (nares) confute (confutabunt), or the eyes (oculi) refute (revincent)? E: will the eyes correct the ears, or the ears the touch? Will taste accuse touch; will nostrils or eyes refute it? DIFF: in the first two questions subject and object are reversed.
489-493. L: it is not (non) so. For to each (cuique) a separate power has been allotted (divisast), each has its own force; and so it must be (necesse est) that the soft, cold or hot is perceived (videre, text doubtful), and the various colors are perceived (sentire) apart, and whatever is joined (coniuncta sint) to colors. E: same, with the infinitives rendered as passives and the textual point noted. MATCH
494-496. L: taste (sapor) likewise has (habet) its force apart, smells are born (nascuntur) apart, sounds apart; so it must be (necesse est) that the senses (sensus, nom.) cannot (non possint) convict (convincere) one another (alios alii). E: same. MATCH
497-498. L: nor (nec) will they (ipsi) be able (poterunt) to correct themselves (sese), since (quoniam) equal trust must (debebit) always be held (haberi). E: same. MATCH
499. L: therefore whatever has seemed (visum est) to them (his) at any time (in quoque tempore) is (est) true. E: same. MATCH
FINDINGS
486	MISTRANSLATION	CRITICAL	0.88	"oculos aures reprehendere, an aures / tactus" has oculos (acc.) as object and aures as subject, then tactus (nom.) as subject and aures (acc.) as object ("will the ears correct the eyes, or touch the ears?"), but the English reverses both, making the eyes correct the ears and the ears correct touch (lines 486-487); "Or will the ears be able to correct the eyes, or touch / the ears? Or again will the mouth's taste accuse this touch,"
438	LEXICAL	MINOR	0.50	"supra rorem salis" is the brine, the sea's surface, which is the water line the oars break at, but "the salt spray" places the line in the spray above the water; "For whatever part of the oars is raised above the salt water"
