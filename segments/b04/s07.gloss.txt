907-911
Latin: [indirect q.] quibus modis somnus (subj.) inriget (pres. subj.) quietem per membra + solvat curas animi e pectore → main: edam (1sg fut.) suavidicis potius quam multis versibus; ut: parvus canor cycni est melior quam ille clamor gruum (dispersus modifies clamor) in aetheriis nubibus austri.
English: in what ways sleep floods limbs with rest / loosens cares → I shall set out in sweet-spoken rather than many verses; as small song of swan is better than clamor of cranes scattered in high clouds of the south wind.

1030-1036
Latin: [quibus semen insinuatur, ubi ipsa dies matura (modifies dies) membris creavit (sc. semen)] conveniunt simulacra e corpore quoque, nuntia voltus coloris, qui (masc. sg., face or color) ciet inritans loca turgida; ut profundant fluctus vestemque cruentent.
English: when the day has made it ripe ("ripe" moved to the seed); images come, heralds of face and color, "which stir" (pl., the images now act); so that they pour out and stain.
DIFF: matura transferred from dies to the seed; the singular qui ciet becomes a plural with the images as subject.

FINDINGS
1034	GRAMMAR	MINOR	0.60	"qui ciet inritans" is masculine singular and refers to the face or colour (simulacra is neuter plural), but the English plural "which stir" makes the images the agent; suggested fix: "which stirs and excites the places swollen with much seed,"
938	GRAMMAR	MINOR	0.55	"aer hic idem" (937-938) says it is this same air that strikes the inner part, but the English moves "this same" onto the body; suggested fix: "strikes it, this same air, as it is drawn in and breathed out."
1031	GRAMMAR	MINOR	0.50	"ipsa dies matura … creavit" means the ripe time itself has created the seed in the limbs, but the English reads matura as making the seed ripe; suggested fix: "when ripe time itself has created it in their limbs,"
1015	AMBIGUITY	MINOR	0.50	"gemitus doloribus edunt" means they groan from pain, but the English idiom "for their pains" means "as a reward for their trouble"; suggested fix: "Many fight it out, and give out groans in their pain,"
1026	GRAMMAR	MINOR	0.50	"dolia curta" is plural, but the English has "a cut-down jar"; suggested fix: "Often the cleanly, if beside a pool or cut-down jars"
