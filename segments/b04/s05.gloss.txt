FINDINGS
4.766	MISTRANSLATION	CRITICAL	0.75	"nec dissentit eum mortis letique potitum / iam pridem, quem mens vivom se cernere credit" says memory does not object that the one the mind thinks it sees alive is long since dead; the English inserts "is not the one", so the embedded proposition denies the identity the Latin asserts (4.767 also). Fix 766-767: "and does not protest that this one is in the grip of death and doom / long since, whom the mind believes it sees alive."
4.776	ADDITION	MINOR	0.60	The note on "particularum" claims Lucretius uses the word "here only", but the register records particulae at 4.261 (and 3.665, 3.708); the verse is correct and only the note's claim is false. Fix: "{{NOTE: particulae, Lucretius's diminutive (as at 4.261); the tiny parts are the images themselves, not the first-beginnings}}"
4.638	MISTRANSLATION	MINOR	0.55	"est itaque ut serpens" presents the snake as a consequence and illustration of 636-637 ("and so"), but "for indeed" turns it into a ground; the register's principle 4 treats connectives as structural. Fix: "and so it is that there is a snake which, touched by a man's spittle,"
4.734	ADDITION	MINOR	0.50	The note says the line "repeats 1.135 verbatim in the Latin", but 1.135 reads "morte obita quorum tellus amplectitur ossa" while 4.734 reads "quorum morte obita": the words are the same and the order differs. Fix: "{{NOTE: repeats 1.135 in the Latin, with the words reordered; the English repeats its own line}}"
