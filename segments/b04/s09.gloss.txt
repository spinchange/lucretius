ANALYSIS

**1192-1196**
- Latin: mulier (subj) nec semper suspirat (pres ind act sg), ficto amore; rel. quae iungit (pres) corpus cum corpore, conplexa (ptc → mulier), et tenet, umectans (ptc → mulier) oscula adsuctis labris; nam facit (pres) ex animo saepe, et quaerens (→ mulier) communia gaudia sollicitat (pres) [inf.] spatium decurrere amoris.
- English: woman does not always sigh with feigned love when she joins / holds, wetting kisses with sucking lips; for she does it from the heart, seeking common joys, urges him to run the course.
- MATCH. "His" kisses and "whole" course are small glosses, below threshold.

**1197-1200**
- Latin: nec ratione alia possent (impf subj pl) volucres, armenta, ferae, pecudes, equae subsidere maribus; si non ardet (pres) natura ipsa abundans, quod illarum subat, et retractat Venerem salientum laeta.
- English: nor could [they] submit beneath males, were it not that their nature, being in heat, burns abundantly and gladly answers the Venus of the mounting males.
- MATCH

**1201-1205**
- Latin: nonne vides (2sg) quos voluptas mutua vinxit (perf), ut excrucientur (pres subj pass) in vinclis communibus; cum canes aventes discedere tendunt (pres) divorsi cupide summis viribus; quom haerent (pres) compagibus Veneris.
- English: same structure; dogs longing to part strain apart while stuck fast.
- MATCH

**1206-1207**

**1212b-1217**

**1218-1222**

- English: same. The ptc. "starting from the stock" could be read as attaching to "fathers" rather than first-beginnings, but this is slight.
- MATCH

**1223-1226**
- Latin: inde Venus producit (pres) figuras varia sorte, refert voltus vocesque comasque; quandoquidem haec fiunt de semine certo nihilo magis quam facies, corpora, membra.
- English: "no less than". This renders the intended equivalence, which Bailey and Rouse also read this way.
- MATCH
