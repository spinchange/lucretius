ANALYSIS

**261-264 (Quod super est ... declarat)**
- Latin: [that] mare, flumina, fontes abundare (inf.) umore novo semper, and latices manare perennis; nil opus est verbis (main); decursus aquarum declarat (pres. ind.) undique.
- English: that the sea, rivers and springs always brim with new moisture, and waters flow without end; there is no need of words; the downrush declares it on every side.
- MATCH

**264-268 (sed primum ... diditur omnis)**
- Latin: quicquid aquai primum tollitur (pres. pass.); in summa fit ut umor nihil abundet (result clause); because venti verrentes aequora deminuunt, and sol aetherius retexens radiis [deminuit]; because [umor] diditur supter per terras.
- English: whatever water comes first is taken off; and so on the whole the moisture never overflows; because winds diminish it, and the sun unweaving it; because it is spread out beneath.
- DIFF: aetherius sol (267) is rendered "the sun of the upper air", against the register's "the sun in the ether" (5.267 = 5.389).

**269-272 (percolatur ... undas)**
- Latin: virus percolatur; materies umoris remanat retro; omnis convenit ad caput amnibus; inde fluit agmine dulci; qua via secta semel detulit (perf.) undas liquido pede.
- English: brine strained out; matter of moisture oozes back; all gathers at the heads; flows in a column of sweet water; where a channel, once cut, carried the waves down.
- MATCH

**273-274 (Aera ... horas)**
- Latin: dicam (fut., 1 sg.) aera; qui mutatur (pass.) innumerabiliter corpore toto privas in horas.
- English: I shall speak of air, which is changed in numberless ways in its whole body every hour.
- MATCH

**275-278 (semper enim ... versa)**
- Latin: quod cumque fluit de rebus, id omne fertur in mare aeris; qui nisi retribuat corpora rebus recreetque fluentis (pres. subj.), omnia forent (impf. subj.) resoluta et versa.
- English: as the Latin, an unreal conditional with air as the subject.
- MATCH

**279-280 (haut igitur ... constat)**
- Latin: [aer] haut cessat gigni de rebus et reccidere in res; quoniam constat omnia fluere adsidue.
- English: air never ceases to be born and to fall back; since it is certain all things flow.
- MATCH

**281-283 (Largus ... lumen)**
- Latin: fons luminis, aetherius sol, inrigat caelum candore recenti; suppeditat lumen novo lumine.
- English: the fountain of light, "the sun of the upper air", floods the sky and supplies light with new light.
- DIFF: aetherius sol (281) again departs from the register.

**284-285 (nam primum ... accidit)**
- Latin: quicquid fulgoris primum disperit ei (dat.), quo cumque accidit.
- English: whatever brightness comes first is lost to it, wherever it falls.
- MATCH

**285-293 (id licet ... ipsum)**
- Latin: you may learn this from the fact that as soon as nubes coepere succedere soli and radios lucis inter...rumpere (tmesis = interrumpere, transitive, object radios); then inferior pars horum disperit and terra inumbratur; ut noscas (purpose) res egere splendore novo, quemque iactum perire, res non posse videri ratione alia, ni caput lucis suppeditet.
- English: clouds begin to pass beneath the sun and "to break through between the rays of light"; the lower part of those rays is lost; earth is shadowed; so that you may know, and so on.
- DIFF: in 287 the clouds interrupt or break the rays (inter-rumpere radios). The English makes rumpere intransitive, so the clouds break through between the rays and the rays are no longer what is broken.

**294-301 (quin etiam ... flammae)**
- Latin: lumina nocturna (lychni, taedae) properant suppeditare novom lumen consimili ratione, ardore ministro (abl. abs.); instant tremere ignibus; lux non relinquit loca, quasi rupta inter; ei (the light) exitium celeratur ab omnibus ignibus, origine flammae.
- English: lamps and torches hurry to supply new light; press on; the light leaves no place as if broken; its destruction is hastened by the swift birth of flame.
- MATCH ("from all its fires" adds "its" to the fires, which is minor)

**302-305 (sic igitur ... vigere)**
- Latin: putandum (gerundive) solem, lunam, stellas iactare lucem ex alio atque alio subortu and perdere quicquid flammarum primum; ne credas (purpose) haec vigere inviolabilia.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**306-310 (Denique non lapides ... niti)**
- Latin: non cernis (rhetorical question) lapides vinci ab aevo, turris ruere, saxa putrescere, delubra et simulacra fatisci, nec numen posse protollere finis fati neque niti adversus foedera naturae?
- English: same, with the negation on posse kept.
- MATCH

**311-317 (denique non monimenta ... fragore)**
- Latin: non videmus monimenta dilapsa (312 corrupt), silices ruere, nec perferre patique vires aevi finiti?; neque enim caderent (impf. subj.) avolsa, quae pertolerassent (plpf. subj.) omnia tormenta ex infinito tempore, privata fragore.
- English: same; the relative "quae pertolerassent" is recast as "if ... they had borne up", which is equivalent.
- MATCH

**318-321 (Denique iam tuere ... constat)**
- Latin: tuere (imperative) hoc, quod continet terram amplexu; si procreat omnia and recipit perempta, totum constat nativum, mortali corpore.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**322-323 (nam quod cumque ... res)**
- Latin: quod cumque auget alitque res, debet deminui, recreari cum recipit.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**324-327 (Praeterea si ... poetae)**
- Latin: si nulla fuit origo genitalis and semper aeterna fuere, cur non cecinere (perf.) alii poetae alias res supera bellum Thebanum?
- English: same.
- MATCH

**328-329 (quo tot ... florent)**
- Latin: quo cecidere facta virum; neque usquam florent insita monimentis?
- English: same.
- MATCH

**330-331 (verum ... cepit)**
- Latin: summa habet novitatem; natura mundi recens est; neque pridem exordia cepit.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**332-337 (quare etiam ... voces)**
- Latin: artes expoliuntur, augescunt; addita sunt multa navigiis; organici peperere sonores; natura rerum ratioque reperta est nuper; ego primus repertus sum qui possim vertere.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**338-344 (Quod si ... futurum)**
- Latin: si credis eadem fuisse, saecla periisse, urbis cecidisse, amnes exisse et coperuisse; tanto magis victus fateare necesse est exitium futurum.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**345-347 (nam cum ... ruinas)**
- Latin: cum res temptarentur morbis periclisque; si incubuisset tristior causa, darent cladem ruinasque.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**348-350 (nec ratione ... removit)**
- Latin: videmur esse mortales nulla ratione alia, nisi quod aegrescimus isdem morbis atque illi quos natura removit.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**351-363 (Praeterea ... plaga)**
- Latin: three alternatives for what is eternal: (a) because it is of solid body, respuere ictus, nec pati penetrare; (b) durare quia expertia plagarum, like the void; (c) because nulla copia loci circum, like summa summarum, with no locus extra and no corpora to incidere.
- English: same three alternatives with the same subjects.
- MATCH

**364-372 (at neque ... perire)**
- Latin: neither is natura mundi of solid body (since inane admixtum), nor is it like the void; neque corpora desunt quae possint corruere summam aut cladem inportare; nec deficit natura loci quo possint moenia exspargi aut [quo] possunt perire pulsa.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**373-375 (haut igitur ... hiatu)**
- Latin: ianua leti non praeclusa est caelo, soli, terrae, undis; sed patet et respectat hiatu.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**376-379 (quare etiam ... vires)**
- Latin: necesse est confiteare haec eadem etiam nativa; neque enim quae sunt mortali corpore potuissent contemnere vires aevi ex infinito tempore.
- English: same. "Etiam" is dropped, which is minor.
- MATCH
