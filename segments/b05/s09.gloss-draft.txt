ANALYSIS

**1105-1107**
- Latin: hi (qui ingenio praestabant et corde vigebant) monstrabant [impf ind act pl], inque dies magis, commutare [inf] victum vitamque priorem novis rebus et igni.
- English: those who excelled... showed, day by day more, how to exchange former food and life for new things and fire.
- MATCH

**1108-1109**
- Latin: reges coeperunt [perf pl] condere urbis arcemque locare, praesidium perfugiumque ipsi sibi (predicative).
- English: kings began to found cities and set up a citadel as defense and refuge for themselves.
- MATCH

**1110-1111**
- Latin: (reges) divisere atque dedere pecudes et agros, pro facie cuiusque et viribus ingenioque.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**1112**
- Latin: nam facies multum valuit, viresque vigebant.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**1113-1114**
- Latin: res inventast aurumque repertum [perf pass]; quod (aurum/res) facile dempsit honorem validis et pulchris.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**1115-1116**
- Latin: enim fortes et pulchro corpore creti, quamlubet, secuntur plerumque sectam divitioris [gen sg].
- English: men, however strong and fair, follow the train of the richer.
- MATCH ("they" in 1116 is loosely anchored, but it reads as the followers.)

**1117-1119**
- Latin: quod si quis gubernet [pres subj] vitam vera ratione, divitiae grandes sunt homini vivere parce aequo animo; neque enim est umquam penuria parvi.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**1120-1122**
- Latin: at voluerunt se claros homines atque potentes, ut fortuna maneret fundamento stabili et (ut) opulenti possent degere placidam vitam.
- English: same, with homines made the subject. This is harmless.
- MATCH

**1123-1126**
- Latin: ne quiquam, quoniam certantes succedere ad summum honorem fecere iter viai infestum; et tamen invidia, quasi fulmen, deicit ictos e summo interdum contemptim in Tartara taetra.
- English: same.
- MATCH

**1127-1128**
- Latin: quoniam invidia, ceu fulmine, vaporant [intr pl] summa et quae cumque sunt aliis magis edita, plerumque.
- English: summits are scorched by envy as by thunderbolt, and whatever stands raised above the rest.
- MATCH

**1129-1130**
- Latin: ut satius multo iam sit parere quietum quam velle regere imperio res et regna tenere.
- English: same, with "rule the world" for regere res. "The world" is a slight widening of res (the state, affairs), below the threshold.
- MATCH

**1131-1132**

**1133-1135**

**1136-1139**

**1161-1168**
- Latin: non ita difficilest reddere rationem verbis (of) quae causa pervulgarit numina deum per magnas gentis, et compleverit urbis ararum, et curarit sollemnia sacra suscipienda, quae sacra florent nunc in magnis rebus locisque, unde etiam nunc est insitus mortalibus horror, qui suscitat nova delubra deum toto orbi terrarum et cogit celebrare festis diebus.
- English: same.
- MATCH ("give an account" departs from the register's "give a reasoning" for rationem reddere, but the sense is the same.)

**1175-1178**

**1179-1182**

**1204-1210**
- Latin: nam cum suspicimus caelestia templa magni mundi (super) et aethera fixum stellis micantibus, et venit in mentem viarum solis lunaeque, tunc illa cura quoque, oppressa aliis malis, infit erigere in pectora caput expergefactum, ne forte quae inmensa potestas deum sit nobis, quae verset candida sidera vario motu.
- English: same, with oppressa taken with pectora rather than cura. Both constructions are grammatically possible.
- MATCH

**1211-1217**
- Latin: enim egestas rationis temptat dubiam mentem, ecquaenam fuerit genitalis origo mundi, et simul ecquae sit finis, quoad moenia mundi et taciti motus possint ferre hunc laborem, an, donata divinitus aeterna salute, possint labentia perpetuo tractu aevi contemnere validas viris inmensi aevi.
- English: same.
- MATCH (Sense is kept. 1216 and 1217 do not carry the register's fixed wording: "an everlasting tract of age", and 5.1217 = 5.379 "the strong forces of immeasurable age".)

**1236-1240**
- Latin: denique cum tellus tota vacillat sub pedibus, concussaeque urbes cadunt dubiaeque minantur, quid mirum si mortalia saecla se temnunt atque relinquunt in rebus magnas mirasque potestatis, viris divum, quae cuncta gubernent [subj]?
- English: same, with "leave room for" for relinquunt (leave in the world, admit). This is a defensible gloss.
- MATCH
