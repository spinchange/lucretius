 1105  And day by day they showed more and more how to exchange
 1106  their former food and life for new things and for fire,
 1107  those who excelled in talent and were vigorous in heart.
 1108  Kings began to found cities and to set up a citadel
 1109  as a defense and a refuge for themselves,
 1110  and they divided flocks and fields and gave them out
 1111  according to each man's looks and strength and talent;
 1112  for looks counted for much, and strength was strong.
 1113  Later, property was invented and gold discovered,
 1114  which easily robbed the strong and the beautiful of their honor;
 1115  for, as a rule, men follow in the train of the richer,
 1116  however strong they may be, however fair of body.
 1117  But if one were to steer his life by true reasoning,
 1118  it is great riches for a man to live sparingly
 1119  with a level mind; for there is never any want of a little.
 1120  But men wanted themselves famous and powerful,
 1121  so that their fortune might rest on a firm foundation
 1122  and they, being wealthy, might pass a calm life:
 1123  in vain, since, struggling to climb to the highest honor,
 1124  they made the road they traveled full of danger,
 1125  and even so, from the summit, like a thunderbolt, it strikes and hurls them,
 1126  envy does, at times, contemptuously, into foul Tartarus;
 1127  since by envy, as by the thunderbolt, the summits are scorched
 1128  as a rule, and whatever stands raised above the rest;
 1129  so that it is far better now to obey in quiet
 1130  than to want to rule the world by command and hold kingdoms.
 1131  So let them sweat blood, worn out, to no purpose,
 1132  struggling along the narrow road of ambition;
 1133  since they are wise from another's mouth, and seek
 1134  things from what they hear rather than from their own senses,
 1135  and this is no more so now, nor will it be soon, than it was before.
 1136  Therefore, the kings slain, there lay overthrown
 1137  the ancient majesty of thrones and the proud scepters,
 1138  and the glorious emblem of the highest head, bloodied,
 1139  beneath the feet of the mob mourned its great honor;
 1140  for what was too much feared before is eagerly trampled.
 1141  And so things went back to the lowest dregs and turmoil,
 1142  when each man sought power and supremacy for himself.
 1143  Then some of them taught men to create magistrates
 1144  and to establish rights, so that they would consent to use laws.
 1145  For the human race, worn out with living its life by force,
 1146  was languishing from its feuds; all the more readily
 1147  it fell of its own accord beneath laws and tight bonds of right.
 1148  For since each man in his anger was preparing to avenge himself
 1149  more fiercely than is now allowed by equal laws,
 1150  for this cause men grew sick of living their life by force.
 1151  From then on, fear of punishment taints the prizes of life.
 1152  For violence and wrong catch each man in their net
 1153  and mostly turn back upon the one from whom they arose,
 1154  nor is it easy to pass a calm and peaceful life
 1155  for one who by his deeds violates the common covenants of peace.
 1156  For even if he escapes the notice of the race of gods and men,
 1157  still he must doubt that it will stay hidden forever;
 1158  since indeed many, speaking often in their dreams
 1159  or raving in sickness, are said to have betrayed themselves
 1160  and brought hidden misdeeds and sins into the open.
 1161  Now, what cause spread the powers of the gods through great nations
 1162  and filled the cities with altars
 1163  and saw to the undertaking of solemn rites,
 1164  rites which now flourish in great affairs and places,
 1165  from which even now a shuddering is planted in mortals
 1166  that raises new shrines of the gods over the whole round
 1167  of earth and drives men to throng them on festal days,
 1168  this is not so hard to give an account of in words.
 1169  For indeed, already then, the mortal generations
 1170  would see glorious forms of gods with waking mind,
 1171  and more in sleep, of marvelous stature of body.
 1172  To these, therefore, they gave sensation, because
 1173  they seemed to move their limbs and to utter proud
 1174  words to match their glorious looks and ample strength.
 1175  And they gave them eternal life, because their
 1176  appearance was always supplied anew and their form remained,
 1177  and yet above all because, endowed with such strength,
 1178  they thought they could not lightly be overcome by any force.
 1179  And so they thought them far beyond others in happiness,
 1180  because the fear of death never troubled any of them,
 1181  and at the same time because in sleep they saw them do
 1182  many marvelous things and yet take no labor from it themselves.
 1183  Further, they saw the systems of heaven in fixed order
 1184  and the various seasons of the years come round,
 1185  and could not learn by what causes this came about.
 1186  And so they made it their refuge to hand everything over
 1187  to the gods, and to make all things bend at their nod.
 1188  And in heaven they placed the seats and precincts of the gods,
 1189  because through heaven night and the moon are seen to roll,
 1190  the moon, day and night, and night's austere signs,
 1191  and the night-wandering torches of heaven, and the flying flames,
 1192  clouds, sun, rains, snow, winds, thunderbolts, hail,
 1193  and the swift roarings and the great murmurs of threat.
 1194  O unhappy human race, when it ascribed such deeds
 1195  to the gods and joined to them bitter angers!
 1196  What groans they brought forth then for themselves, what wounds
 1197  for us, what tears for those who come after us!
 1198  Nor is it any piety to be seen often, with veiled head, {{NOTE: the Roman worshipper covered the head at sacrifice}}
 1199  turning toward a stone and drawing near to every altar,
 1200  nor to lie prostrate on the ground and spread the palms
 1201  before the shrines of the gods, nor to sprinkle the altars
 1202  with much blood of four-footed beasts, nor to link vows to vows,
 1203  but rather to be able to look on all things with a mind at peace.
 1204  For when we look up at the heavenly precincts of the great
 1205  world, and the ether fixed above with glittering stars,
 1206  and the thought of the ways of sun and moon comes into the mind,
 1207  then into hearts weighed down with other evils that care
 1208  too begins to raise its head, roused awake:
 1209  whether there may perhaps be some immeasurable power of the gods
 1210  over us, to turn the shining stars in their varied motion;
 1211  for poverty of reasoning assails the doubting mind,
 1212  whether there was ever any generative origin of the world,
 1213  and also whether there is some end, how long the walls of the world
 1214  and their silent motion can bear this labor,
 1215  or whether, gifted by the gods with everlasting safety,
 1216  they can glide on through the endless stretch of age
 1217  and scorn the mighty strength of immeasurable age.
 1218  Further, whose mind does not shrink with fear of the gods,
 1219  whose limbs do not creep with terror,
 1220  when beneath the horrible stroke of the thunderbolt the scorched earth
 1221  quakes, and murmurs run across the great sky?
 1222  Do not peoples and nations tremble, and proud kings
 1223  shrink in their limbs, struck by fear of the gods,
 1224  lest for some foul deed or proudly spoken word
 1225  the heavy time of paying the penalty has come to its full?
 1226  Again, when the utmost force of a violent wind over the sea
 1227  sweeps the commander of a fleet across the waters
 1228  together with his mighty legions and his elephants,
 1229  does he not court the peace of the gods with vows, and in prayer beg,
 1230  in his fear, for calms of the winds and favoring breezes?
 1231  In vain, since often, caught up in a violent whirlwind,
 1232  he is carried none the less to the shoals of death.
 1233  So utterly does some hidden force
 1234  crush human affairs, and the fair rods and the cruel axes {{NOTE: the fasces and axes carried before Roman magistrates, emblems of their power}}
 1235  it seems to trample underfoot and hold in mockery.
 1236  Finally, when the whole earth sways beneath our feet,
 1237  and cities fall, shaken, or threaten, unsteady, to fall,
 1238  what wonder if mortal generations despise themselves
 1239  and leave room for great and marvelous powers,
 1240  strengths of the gods in the world, to steer all things?
