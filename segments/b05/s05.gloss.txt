ANALYSIS
614-619: Latin: ratio solis (subj.) nec patescit (pres. ind., neg.) simplex et recta; indirect questions: quo pacto [sol] adeat (subj.) brumalis flexus aegocerotis e partibus aestivis, atque revertens ut vertat (subj.) ad metas solstitialis canceris; lunaque videatur (subj. pass.) obire spatium mensibus; in quo sol consumit (ind.) tempora annua cursu. English: the order of the sun does not lie open, simple and straight; how it comes to Capricorn's winter turnings; how, turning back, it bends toward Cancer's solstice posts; how the moon is seen to pass through that space in a month; in which the sun spends a year. MATCH.
620: Latin: non reddita causa est (perf. pass.) simplex his rebus; inquam. English: not one simple cause is given, I say. MATCH.
621-628: Latin: videtur id posse fieri vel cum primis; quod sententia Democriti ponit; quanto magis sidera sint (subj.) prope terram, tanto posse minus ferri cum turbine caeli; enim vires illius evanescere et imminui supter; ideo solem relinqui paulatim cum signis posterioribus; quod sit (subj.) inferior quam signa fervida. English: this seems able above all to happen; what Democritus' judgment lays down; the nearer each star, the less it can be carried with the whirl; its forces fade and lessen below; so the sun is left behind with the hinder signs; because it is lower than the burning signs. MATCH.
629-631: Latin: magis hoc lunam [relinqui]; quanto cursus demissior abest a caelo et propinquat terris, tanto posse minus tendere cursum cum signis. English: the moon still more; the lower its course, the farther from the sky and nearer to the earth, the less it can keep its course level with the signs. MATCH (correlative recast, sense kept).
632-634: Latin: quanto turbine flaccidiore fertur (pres. pass.) inferior quam sol, tanto magis omnia signa adipiscuntur hanc et praeterferuntur. English: the more sluggish the whirl that carries it, lower than the sun, the more the signs overtake it and are carried past. MATCH.
635-636: Latin: fit ut haec videatur reverti mobilius ad signum quodque, quia signa revisunt ad hanc. English: it comes about that it seems to return more quickly to each sign, because the signs come back to it. MATCH.
637-642: Latin: fit ut aer alter possit fluere alternis certo tempore e partibus transversis mundi; qui queat detrudere solem a signis aestivis usque ad flexus brumalis; et qui reiciat [solem] a frigoris umbris usque in partis aestiferas. English: may be that an air can flow at a fixed time, by turns, from cross regions; one to thrust the sun from the summer signs to the winter turnings; one to fling it back from cold shades to the heat-bearing parts. MATCH.
643-645: Latin: putandum est (gerundive) lunam stellasque, quae volvunt annos, posse ire aeribus e partibus alternis. English: we must suppose the moon and stars, which roll great years, can travel by airs from alternate parts. MATCH.
646-647: Latin: nonne vides nubila ire in partis diversas ventis diversis, inferna supernis? English: do you not see clouds go in contrary directions under contrary winds, lower against upper? MATCH.
648-649: Latin: qui minus illa sidera queant ferri aestibus diversis per orbis aetheris? English: why should those stars be less able to be carried on contrary currents through the orbits of ether? MATCH.
650-655: Latin: nox obruit terras caligine; aut ubi sol impulit ultima caeli atque languidus efflavit ignis suos concussos itere et labefactos aere (participles agree with ignis); aut quia vis eadem cogit convortere cursum sub terras, quae pertulit orbem supra terras. English: night buries the lands; either when the sun has struck the farthest parts and, faint, breathed out its fires, shaken and weakened; or because the same force compels it to turn its course beneath the earth, the force that carried its orb above. MATCH (participle attachment slightly loose but placed after "fires").
656-662: Latin: Matuta differt auroram per oras aetheris et pandit lumina; aut quia sol idem revertens anticipat caelum temptans accendere radiis; aut quia ignes conveniunt et semina ardoris consuerunt confluere tempore certo, quae faciunt lumina solis gigni nova. English: Matuta spreads dawn and opens the light; either because the same sun, returning, seizes the sky beforehand trying to kindle it; or because fires come together and seeds of heat have been accustomed to flow together, which cause the sun's light to be born new. MATCH.
663-665: Latin: fama est ignis dispersos cerni e montibus Idaeis lumine orienti, inde coire in globum et conficere orbem. English: the story goes scattered fires are seen from Ida at the rising light, then gather into a ball and make an orb. MATCH.
666-668: Latin: nec illud debet esse mirabile, quod haec semina ignis possint confluere certo tempore et reparare nitorem solis. English: nor should this be a wonder, that these seeds can flow together at so fixed a time and restore the sun's brightness. MATCH.
669-670: Latin: videmus enim multa quae fiunt certo tempore in omnibus rebus. English: for we see many things that come about at a fixed time in every kind of thing. MATCH.
670-671: Latin: arbusta florescunt et dimittunt florem certo tempore. English: the trees blossom and let their flower fall at a fixed time. MATCH.
672-674: Latin: aetas imperat dentes cadere certo tempore, et inpubem pubescere molli veste, et pariter demittere (let hang down, grow) mollem barbam malis. English: age commands teeth to fall, the beardless boy to grow his soft down, and alike to let a soft beard fall down from his cheeks. DIFF: "fall down from" reads as shedding the beard, where demittere is letting it grow down.
675-676: Latin: fulmina, nix, imbres, nubila, venti fiunt in partibus anni non nimis incertis. English: thunderbolts, snow, rains, clouds, winds come about at seasons not too uncertain. MATCH.
677-679: Latin: namque ubi exordia causarum sic fuerunt atque res mundi ita cecidere ab origine prima, [consequiae] redeunt ex ordine certo. English: for since the first starting-points of causes were thus and things fell out so from the first origin of the world, in sequence they come back in a fixed order. MATCH.
680-688: Latin: licet dies crescere, noctes tabescere, luces minui cum sumant augmina noctis; aut quia sol currens amfractibus imparibus partit oras aetheris et dividit orbem in partis non aequas, et reponit in adversa parte quod detraxit ab alterutra, relatus tanto plus, donec pervenit ad signum ubi nodus anni exaequat umbras lucibus. English: same clauses, same subjects. MATCH.
689-695: Latin: nam caelum distinet metas aequato discrimine medio cursu flatus aquilonis et austri, propter posituram orbis signiferi, in quo sol serpens concludit tempora annua, lustrans terras et caelum lumine obliquo, ut ratio declarat eorum qui notarunt loca caeli ornata signis. English: heaven holds its turning-posts apart at an equal distance midway between north and south winds, because of the position of the sign-bearing circle in which the sun, creeping, encloses its yearly times, lighting earth and heaven slantwise, as those declare who marked the regions adorned with signs. MATCH.
696-700: Latin: aut quia aer crassior est certis in partibus, ideo iubar ignis haesitat sub terris nec potest penetrare et emergere ad ortus; propterea noctes longae cessant hiberno tempore dum veniat insigne diei. English: or because the air is thicker, and so the beam hesitates beneath the earth and cannot pass through and rise; therefore in winter the long nights linger until the badge of day arrives. MATCH.
701-704: Latin: aut quia ignes, qui faciunt solem surgere certa de parte, consuerunt confluere tardius et citius alternis partibus anni, propterea fit ut videantur dicere verum. English: or because the fires that make the sun rise from a fixed quarter have been wont to flow together slower and quicker in alternate parts of the year, therefore they seem to speak the truth. MATCH.
705-714: Latin: luna potest nitere percussa radiis solis, et convertere lumen ad speciem nobis magis in dies, quantum secedit ab orbi solis, donec fulsit pleno lumine contra eum et oriens vidit obitus eius; inde debet condere lumen retro, quanto propius labitur ad ignem solis ex alia parte signorum; ut faciunt qui fingunt lunam consimilem pilai et tenere viam sub sole. English: same structure. MATCH.
715-719: Latin: est quare possit volvier proprio lumine et reddere formas splendoris; enim licet esse corpus aliud quod fertur et labitur una occursans officiensque, nec potis est cerni quia fertur cassum lumine. English: same. MATCH.
720-726: Latin: potest versari, ut globus pilai tinctus lumine dimidia ex parte, et versando globum edere formas variantis, donec vertit partem ignibus auctam ad speciem nobis; inde contorquet et aufert partem luciferam. English: same. MATCH.
727-730: Latin: ut doctrina Chaldaeum refutans artem astrologorum tendit convincere contra, quasi id nequeat fieri quod uterque pugnat, aut minus sit cur ausis amplectier hoc illo. English: same. MATCH.
731-736: Latin: difficile est docere ratione et vincere verbis cur nequeat nova luna creari semper ordine certo, et quaeque creata aborisci in dies privos, et alia reparari in parte locoque, cum videas multa creari ordine tam certo. English: same. MATCH.
737-740: Latin: it Ver et Venus, et praenuntius Veneris pennatus graditur ante; prope vestigia Zephyri Flora mater, quibus praespargens ante viai, opplet cuncta coloribus et odoribus. English: same. MATCH.
741-742: Latin: sequitur Calor aridus et una comes Ceres pulverulenta et flabra etesia aquilonum. English: same. MATCH.
743: Latin: Autumnus adit, graditur simul Euhius Euan. English: same. MATCH.
744-745: Latin: aliae tempestates ventique secuntur, Volturnus altitonans et Auster fulmine pollens. English: same. MATCH.
746-747: Latin: Bruma adfert nives et reddit rigorem pigrum; Hiemps sequitur hanc crepitans dentibus algu. English: same. MATCH.
748-750: Latin: quo minus est mirum si luna gignitur et deletur rursus certo tempore, cum multa possint fieri tam certo tempore. English: same. MATCH.

FINDINGS
674	MISTRANSLATION	MODERATE	0.60	"mollem malis demittere barbam" is letting a soft beard grow down from the cheeks, but "let a soft beard fall down from his cheeks", beside the falling teeth (672) and falling flowers (671), reads as shedding the beard; "and alike to let a soft beard hang down from his cheeks."
