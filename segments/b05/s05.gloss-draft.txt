ANALYSIS

614-619: Latin: ratio solis (subj.) nec patescit (pres. ind., neg.) simplex et recta (predicative); indirect questions quo pacto [sol] adeat (subj.) flexus brumalis aegocerotis, ut vertat (subj.) ad metas solstitialis canceris; luna videatur (subj. pass.) obire spatium; relative: in quo sol consumit annua tempora. English: the reasoning of the sun is not laid open, simple and straight; how it comes, how it bends, how the moon is seen to pass through the space in which the sun spends a year. DIFF: ratio solis is rendered "reasoning", where the register fixes "the order of the sun" for this line.

620: Latin: causa (subj.) non reddita est (perf. pass.) simplex. English: not one simple cause is given. MATCH.

621-624: Latin: id videtur (pres.) posse fieri cum primis; relative: quod sententia Democriti ponit; acc.+inf.: quanto sidera sint (subj.) propter terram, tanto [ea] posse minus ferri cum turbine caeli. English: same structure, nearer/less carried. MATCH.

625-628: Latin: acc.+inf.: rapidas et acris viris illius (sc. turbinis) evanescere et imminui supter; solem relinqui paulatim cum posterioribus signis; causal: quod sit (subj.) inferior quam fervida signa. English: forces of the whirl fade and lessen; the sun is left behind; because it is lower. MATCH.

629-631: Latin: et magis hoc lunam [relinqui]; quanto demissior eius cursus abest procul a caelo terrisque propinquat (one quanto clause, two verbs, subj. cursus), tanto [lunam] posse minus tendere cursum cum signis. English: "the lower its course, / the farther it lies ... and the nearer it comes ..., / the less it can". The English makes a chain of three correlatives. DIFF: the single quanto clause is split into "the lower ..., the farther ..." as if it were cause and effect.

632-634: Latin: quanto fertur (pres. pass.) flaccidiore turbine, inferior quam sol, tanto magis omnia signa hanc adipiscuntur et praeter feruntur. English: same structure. MATCH.

635-636: Latin: fit ut haec videatur reverti mobilius ad quodque signum, quia signa revisunt ad hanc. English: same. MATCH.

637-642: Latin: fit ut aer alter possit fluere alternis certo tempore e transversis partibus; relatives: qui queat detrudere solem ...; qui reiciat [eum] a gelidis umbris ... in aestiferas partis. English: same. MATCH.

643-645: Latin: putandumst (gerundive) ratione pari lunam stellasque (relative: quae volvunt annos) posse ire aeribus alternis e partibus. English: same, but pari ratione is rendered "by like reasoning" where the register fixes "on (by) a like principle" (5.643). DIFF (register only).

646-647: Latin: nonne vides nubila ire (inf.) diversis ventis in diversas partis, inferna supernis? English: same. MATCH.

648-649: Latin: qui minus illa sidera queant (subj.) ferri diversis aestibus? English: same. MATCH.

650-655: Latin: nox obruit terras; aut ubi sol impulit (perf.) ultima caeli atque languidus efflavit ignis, where concussos and labefactos (acc. pl.) modify ignis; aut quia vis eadem cogit [eum] convortere cursum, relative: quae pertulit orbem. English: "has breathed out its fires, / shaken by the journey and weakened by much air". The participles can be read with the sun. DIFF: the modifier of concussos/labefactos is ambiguous.

656-662: Latin: Matuta differt auroram et pandit lumina; aut quia sol anticipat, temptans accendere; aut quia ignes conveniunt et semina consuerunt confluere; relative: quae faciunt lumina gigni. English: same. MATCH.

663-665: Latin: fama est ignis dispersos cerni (pass. inf.), inde coire et conficere orbem. English: same. MATCH.

666-668: Latin: illud non debet esse mirabile, quod semina ignis possint confluere et reparare nitorem. English: same. MATCH.

669-671: Latin: videmus multa quae fiunt certo tempore; arbusta florescunt, dimittunt florem. English: same. MATCH.

672-674: Latin: aetas imperat (pres.) dentes cadere, inpubem pubescere veste, et demittere (let hang down, i.e. grow) barbam malis. English: "to let a soft beard fall down from his cheeks". DIFF: the English reads as shedding the beard, echoing "let their flower fall" (671).

675-676: Latin: fulmina, nix, etc. fiunt partibus anni non nimis incertis. English: same. MATCH.

677-679: Latin: ubi exordia fuerunt sic, atque res mundi cecidere ab origine, [res] redeunt ex ordine certo. English: same, with mundi slightly shifted. MATCH.

680-688: Latin: licet dies crescere, noctes tabescere, luces minui; aut quia sol partit oras et dividit orbem, reponit quod detraxit, donec pervenit ad signum; relative: ubi nodus exaequat umbras. English: same. MATCH.

689-695: Latin: nam caelum distinet metas aequato discrimine, propter posituram orbis; relative: in quo sol concludit tempora, lustrans; ut ratio declarat eorum qui notarunt. English: same. MATCH.

696-700: Latin: aut quia aer crassior est; ideo iubar haesitat nec potest penetrare; propterea noctes longae cessant, dum veniat insigne. English: same. MATCH.

701-704: Latin: aut quia ignes consuerunt confluere tardius et citius; relative: qui faciunt solem surgere; propterea fit uti videantur dicere verum. English: same, with a note on "they". MATCH.

705-712: Latin: luna potest nitere, percussa radiis, et convertere lumen, quantum secedit; donique eum (the sun) contra fulsit atque oriens, super edita, vidit obitus eius (the sun's setting); inde debet condere lumen, quanto labitur propius. English: "has looked down upon its setting". "Its" most naturally means the moon. DIFF: the referent of eius is shifted from the sun to the moon.

713-714: Latin: ut faciunt qui fingunt lunam esse consimilem pilai et tenere viam sub sole. English: same. MATCH.

715-719: Latin: est quare possit volvier proprio lumine et reddere formas; licet corpus aliud esse, quod fertur et labitur, occursans officiensque, nec potis est cerni, quia fertur cassum lumine. English: same. MATCH.

720-726: Latin: [luna] potest versari, ut globus pilai tinctus dimidia parte, et edere formas, donique vertit partem ad speciem; inde contorquet et aufert partem. English: same. MATCH.

727-730: Latin: ut doctrina Chaldaeum, refutans artem astrologorum, tendit convincere contra, quasi id nequeat fieri quod pugnat uterque, aut minus sit cur ausis amplectier. English: same. MATCH.

731-736: Latin: cur nequeat luna creari, quaeque aborisci, alia reparari, difficilest docere et vincere, cum videas multa creari. English: same. MATCH.

737-740: Latin: it Ver et Venus, praenuntius graditur ante; Flora, quibus praespargens viai cuncta, opplet coloribus et odoribus. English: same. MATCH.

741-747: Latin: sequitur Calor, comes Ceres et flabra; Autumnus adit, graditur Euan; tempestates secuntur; Bruma adfert nives, reddit rigorem; Hiemps sequitur crepitans. English: same. MATCH.

748-750: Latin: quo minus est mirum, si luna gignitur et deletur certo tempore, cum multa possint fieri. English: same. MATCH.
