  614  Nor does the order of the sun lie open, simple and straight:
  615  how, from its summer regions, it comes to Capricorn's {{NOTE: aegocerotis, "the goat-horned"; Capricorn and Cancer (617) mark the winter and summer tropics}}
  616  winter turnings, and how, turning back from there,
  617  it bends toward the solstice goal-posts of Cancer,
  618  and how the moon is seen to pass in a month through that space
  619  in which the sun spends the time of a year in its course.
  620  Not one simple cause, I say, is given for these things.
  621  For it seems that this, above all, can come about,
  622  what the holy judgment of the man Democritus lays down:
  623  that the nearer to the earth each of the stars is,
  624  the less it can be carried with the whirl of heaven;
  625  for the swift and fierce forces of that whirl fade away
  626  and lessen down below, and so the sun
  627  is little by little left behind with the hinder signs,
  628  because it is far lower than the burning signs.
  629  And the moon still more: the lower its course,
  630  the farther it is from the sky and the nearer to the earth,
  631  the less it can keep its course level with the signs.
  632  Further, the more sluggish the whirl that carries it,
  633  being lower than the sun, the more do all the signs
  634  overtake it all around and are carried past it.
  635  So it comes about that it seems to return to each sign
  636  more quickly, because the signs come back to it.
  637  It may also be that from the cross regions of the world an air
  638  can flow at a fixed time, by turns, now one, now the other,
  639  one able to thrust the sun out of the summer signs
  640  as far as the winter turnings and their freezing stiffness,
  641  and one to fling it back from the freezing shades of cold
  642  as far as the heat-bearing parts and the burning signs.
  643  And on a like principle we must suppose that the moon and stars,
  644  which roll great years round in great orbits,
  645  can travel by airs from alternate parts.
  646  Do you not see that the clouds, too, under contrary winds,
  647  go in contrary directions, the lower against the upper?
  648  Why should those stars be less able, through the great orbits of ether,
  649  to be carried on currents contrary to one another?
  650  But night buries the lands in huge darkness,
  651  either when the sun, after its long course, has struck the farthest
  652  parts of heaven and, grown faint, has breathed out its fires,
  653  shaken by the journey and weakened by much air,
  654  or because the same force compels it to turn its course beneath
  655  the earth, the force that carried its orb above the lands.
  656  Likewise at a fixed time Matuta spreads rosy dawn {{NOTE: Matuta, the old Italian goddess of the dawn}}
  657  through the regions of the ether and opens wide the light,
  658  either because the same sun, returning beneath the earth,
  659  seizes the sky beforehand, trying to kindle it with its rays,
  660  or because fires come together and many seeds
  661  of heat have been accustomed to flow together at a fixed time,
  662  which cause the sun's light to be born always new;
  663  just as, the story goes, from the high mountains of Ida {{NOTE: Mount Ida near Troy, where scattered fires were said to be seen gathering into the sun at daybreak}}
  664  scattered fires are seen at the rising of the light,
  665  and then gather as if into a single ball and make an orb.
  666  Nor yet should this be a thing to wonder at
  667  in these matters, that these seeds of fire can at so fixed a time
  668  flow together and restore the brightness of the sun.
  669  For we see many things that come about at a fixed time
  670  in every kind of thing. At a fixed time they blossom,
  671  the trees, and at a fixed time they let their flower fall.
  672  No less does age command the teeth to fall at a fixed
  673  time, and the beardless boy to grow his soft clothing of down,
  674  and alike to let a soft beard hang down from his cheeks.
  675  Finally thunderbolts, snow, rains, clouds, winds
  676  come about at seasons of the year not too uncertain.
  677  For since the first starting-points of causes were thus,
  678  and things fell out so from the first origin of the world,
  679  in sequence they come back now, too, in a fixed order. {{NOTE: the source's "conseque" is corrupt; the sense "in sequence" follows the usual correction (consequiae)}}
  680  Likewise the days may lengthen and the nights waste away,
  681  and the daylight lessen, when the nights take increase,
  682  either because the same sun, running beneath the earth and above
  683  in unequal arcs, parts the regions of the ether
  684  and divides its orbit into parts that are not equal,
  685  and what it has taken from the one side it restores,
  686  carried round so much the more, to the opposite side,
  687  until it reaches that sign of heaven where the node
  688  of the year makes the shades of night equal with the light;
  689  for midway in its course between the blasts of north wind and south
  690  heaven holds its turning-posts apart at an equal distance
  691  because of the position of the whole sign-bearing circle, {{NOTE: the zodiac, set slantwise to the celestial equator}}
  692  in which the sun, creeping, encloses its yearly times,
  693  lighting earth and heaven with slanting light,
  694  as the reasoning of those declares who have marked out
  695  all the regions of heaven, adorned with their signs set in order.
  696  Or because the air is thicker in certain parts,
  697  and so beneath the earth the trembling beam of fire hesitates
  698  and cannot easily pass through and come up to its rising;
  699  therefore in wintertime the nights, being long,
  700  linger, until the rayed badge of day arrives.
  701  Or else, because in this way, in alternate parts of the year,
  702  the fires have been wont to flow together slower and quicker,
  703  the fires which make the sun rise from a fixed quarter,
  704  therefore it comes about that they seem to speak the truth. {{NOTE: "they": those who hold that the sun is formed new each day from gathering fires (660-665)}}
  705  The moon may shine because it is struck by the sun's rays,
  706  and turn that light day by day more toward us
  707  into view, the farther it withdraws from the sun's orb,
  708  until, opposite the sun, it has shone with full light
  709  and, rising, has looked down on the sun's setting from the heights;
  710  then little by little, backward, as it were, it must hide
  711  its light in turn, the closer it now glides to the sun's fire
  712  from the other side of the signs, through its orbit;
  713  so hold those who picture the moon to be like
  714  a ball, and to keep the path of its course beneath the sun.
  715  There is reason, too, why it could roll on with its own light
  716  and still give back varied forms of brightness;
  717  for there may be another body, carried along and gliding
  718  with it, in every way meeting and obstructing it,
  719  yet unable to be seen, since it moves bare of light.
  720  Or it may turn, like, perhaps, the globe of a ball
  721  dyed on one half of it with shining light,
  722  and, by turning the globe, give out changing forms,
  723  until it turns that part, whichever is endowed with fires,
  724  into our view and toward our open eyes;
  725  then little by little it twists back and takes away
  726  the light-bearing part of the globed mass, the ball;
  727  as the Babylonian doctrine of the Chaldaeans, refuting {{NOTE: the Chaldaeans, Babylonian astronomers who held the moon half-luminous; the "astronomers" of 728 are the Greeks who held it lit by the sun}}
  728  the art of the astronomers, strives to prove the contrary,
  729  just as if what each side fights for could not come about,
  730  or there were less reason why you should dare embrace this than that.
  731  Finally, why a new moon could not always be created
  732  in a fixed order of forms and with fixed shapes,
  733  and each day each one, once created, perish,
  734  and another be restored in its part and place,
  735  is hard to teach by reasoning and to prove with words,
  736  since you see so many things created in so fixed an order.
  737  Spring goes on her way, and Venus, and Venus's winged herald {{NOTE: Cupid}}
  738  steps on before them; and close upon Zephyr's footprints
  739  mother Flora, strewing all the way before them,
  740  fills everything with surpassing colors and scents.
  741  Next in their place follows parching Heat, and with it as companion
  742  dusty Ceres and the etesian blasts of the north winds. {{NOTE: etesian, the "annual" north winds of the Mediterranean summer}}
  743  Then Autumn comes on, and with it steps Euhius Euan. {{NOTE: Bacchus, under two names from the ritual cry of his worshippers}}
  744  Then other seasons and winds follow,
  745  high-thundering Volturnus and the South Wind strong with lightning. {{NOTE: Volturnus, the south-east wind}}
  746  At last Midwinter brings snows and gives back numbing
  747  stiffness. Winter follows her, teeth chattering with cold.
  748  So it is less to be wondered at if at a fixed time the moon
  749  is born and at a fixed time is blotted out again,
  750  since so many things can come about at so fixed a time.
