1236-1240
L: denique cum tota tellus vacillat sub pedibus concussaeque cadunt urbes dubiaeque minantur, quid mirum si mortalia saecla temnunt se atque relinquunt in rebus potestatis magnas mirasque, viris divum (acc. pl., in apposition), quae cuncta gubernent?
E: when the earth sways and cities fall or threaten, what wonder if mortal generations despise themselves and consign great powers to the strengths of the gods (dative), to steer all things?
DIFF: viris divum read as an indirect object ("to the strengths of the gods") instead of in apposition to potestatis; relinquunt ("leave in the world") becomes "consign ... to".

FINDINGS
1240	GRAMMAR	MODERATE	0.72	"relinquunt potestatis magnas mirasque / in rebus viris divum": viris is accusative plural, in apposition to potestatis (mortals leave in the world mighty powers, the strengths of the gods, to govern all), but the English reads it as a dative recipient and turns relinquunt into "consign", so powers are handed to the gods' strengths; fix 1239-1240 as "and leave in the world great and marvelous powers, / the strengths of the gods, to steer all things?"
