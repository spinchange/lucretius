ANALYSIS

**495-497**
- Latin: pondus (terrae, concreto corpore abl.) constitit; atque [omnis mundi quasi limus] confluxit gravis in imum et subsedit funditus ut faex. omnis is gen. with mundi ("of the whole world") or nom. masc. with limus. It cannot agree with neuter pondus. gravis is nom. masc., with limus.
- English: weight of earth settled; "all of it, heavy, like the slime of the world" flowed together and sank like dregs.
- DIFF: omnis is read as "all of it" (the earth's weight); "whole" is lost from "of the whole world".

**498-505**
- Latin:
  - mare, aer, aether ignifer sunt omnia pura relicta (corporibus liquidis abl.), leviora aliis alia.
  - aether liquidissimus levissimusque super influit auras, nec commiscet corpus auris turbantibus.
  - sinit haec verti turbinibus; sinit [haec] turbare (intrans.) procellis.
  - ipse fert ignis certo impete labens.
- English: same subjects and verbs; "lets them riot" for turbare intransitive; "below" is a gloss.
- MATCH

- MATCH ("evenly" for modice is close, but defensible)

**517-525**
- Latin:

  - Reason 1: sive quod aetheris aestus inclusi sunt, quaerentes viam versantur, et volvunt ignes per summania templa.
  - Reason 2: sive aer extrinsecus fluens versat agens ignis.
  - Reason 3: sive ipsi [ignes] serpere possunt quo cibus vocat invitatque euntis, pascentis corpora (pascentis agrees with ignes).
- English: same. "Out" is implicit in quaerentes viam.
- MATCH

FINDINGS
496	GRAMMAR	MINOR	0.55	"omnis mundi quasi limus ... gravis" makes omnis either genitive with mundi ("of the whole world") or nominative with limus, and it cannot agree with neuter pondus; the English "all of it, heavy" makes it refer to the earth's weight and loses "whole" from the world (see also 495, 497). Fix: "settled, and like the slime of the whole world, heavy, into the depths"
