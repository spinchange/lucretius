ANALYSIS

1241-1251
Latin: aes, aurum, ferrum repertumst + argenti pondus, plumbi potestas (subj.); temporal ubi: ignis (subj.) cremarat (plpf. ind. act.) silvas; seu fulmine misso (abl. abs.); sive quod [homines] gerentes bellum intulerant (plpf.) ignem hostibus formidinis ergo; sive quod inducti volebant pandere agros, reddere rura pascua; sive [volebant] interficere feras, ditescere praeda; nam venarier (inf. subj.) prius est ortum quam saepire saltum plagis canibusque ciere.
English: bronze, gold, iron were discovered + weight of silver, power of lead; when fire had burned forests; whether thunderbolt sent; or because men waging war had carried fire to cause terror; or because drawn on they wished to open fields, turn countryside to pasture; or to kill beasts, grow rich; for hunting arose earlier than fencing and rousing.
MATCH

1252-1257 (to plumbi)
Latin: quicquid id est; ardor (subj.) exederat silvas a radicibus, percoxerat terram; manabat (impf.) rivus (subj.) argenti et auri, aeris et plumbi, venis ferventibus, in loca concava, conveniens.
English: flaming heat had eaten forests from roots, baked earth; there flowed from seething veins into hollow places, running together, a stream of silver, gold, bronze, lead.
MATCH

1257-1261
Latin: cum [homines] videbant quae concreta splendere; tollebant capti lepore; et videbant [ea] formata esse simili figura atque lacunarum vestigia fuerant cuique.
English: when they saw these hardened, shining; they picked them up, captured by charm; saw each was shaped like the print of its hollow.
MATCH

1262-1268
Latin: penetrabat eos: haec liquefacta posse decurrere in quamlibet formam; posse duci in fastigia mucronum procudendo; purpose ut parent tela, ut possint caedere, dolare, radere, terebrare, pertundere, forare.
English: it entered their minds that these, melted, could run into any form; could be drawn into points by hammering; so they might make weapons, be able to cut, hew, plane, bore, punch, pierce.
MATCH

1269-1272
Latin: [homines] parabant facere haec argento auroque nec minus quam viribus aeris; ne quiquam, quoniam cedebat victa potestas [metallorum] nec poterant (subj. = argentum aurumque) sufferre laborem.
English: they set about doing these with silver and gold no less than with bronze; in vain, since "their" power gave way and [they] could not endure the labor.
DIFF: the only antecedent for "their"/"could not" is the men, not the metals.

1273-1275
Latin: aes fuit in pretio magis; aurum iacebat propter inutilitatem, retusum mucrone; nunc iacet aes, aurum successit in honorem.
English: bronze in higher esteem, gold lay neglected for uselessness, blunted; now bronze lies, gold has risen.
