ANALYSIS

136-139
- Latin skeleton: dico [inf.] animum atque animam coniuncta teneri inter se (pass.) + unam naturam conficere ex se; sed consilium (subj.) caput esse quasi et dominari in corpore toto; rel. quod nos animum mentemque vocamus (pres. ind. 1pl).
- English skeleton: I say mind and spirit are held joined, make up one nature; but the head/lord over whole body is the counsel; which we call mind and thought.
- MATCH. Dominari is rendered as the appositive "lord", which keeps the sense.

140
- Latin skeleton: id (consilium) situm haeret (pres. ind.) media regione pectoris.
- English skeleton: this is set and fixed in the middle region of the breast.
- MATCH.

141-142
- Latin skeleton: enim; hic exultat pavor ac metus; circum haec loca laetitiae mulcent; ergo hic mens animusque est.
- English skeleton: for here dread and fear leap up; around these places joys caress; therefore here are thought and mind.
- MATCH.

143-144
- Latin skeleton: cetera pars animae (dissita per totum corpus) paret et movetur (pass.) ad numen momenque mentis.
- English skeleton: the rest of the spirit, scattered, obeys and moves at the mind's nod and impulse.
- MATCH.

145-146
- Latin skeleton: id (consilium) solum per se sapit et sibi gaudet; cum-clause: neque animam neque corpus res commovet una.
- English skeleton: this alone knows and is glad for itself; when nothing moves spirit or body along with it.
- MATCH.

147-151
- Latin skeleton: quasi cum caput aut oculus laeditur (pass.) temptante dolore (abl. abs.), non concruciamur omni corpore; sic animus laeditur ipse laetitiaque viget nonnumquam; cum cetera pars animai nulla novitate cietur (pass.).
- English skeleton: as when head or eye is hurt by attacking pain, we are not tormented in the whole body; so mind is sometimes hurt or thrives; when the rest of spirit is stirred by nothing new.
- MATCH. "or" for -que is defensible.

152-160
- Latin skeleton: ubi mens commota est metu vementi magis; videmus [inf.] consentire animam totam, sudores palloremque existere, infringi linguam, vocem aboriri, caligare oculos, sonere auris, succidere artus; denique videmus concidere homines saepe; result: ut quivis noscere possit (subj.) esse animam coniunctam cum animo; rel. quae, cum percussa est vi animi, exim propellit et icit corpus.
- English skeleton: when mind is moved by more violent fear, we see the whole spirit feel with it; sweat, pallor, faltering tongue, failing voice, dark eyes, ringing ears, limbs giving way; finally we see men collapse; so that anyone may learn the spirit is joined with mind and, struck by mind's force, drives and strikes the body.
- MATCH. The symptoms are moved out from under "we see" with no change of sense.

161-167
- Latin skeleton: haec ratio docet naturam animi atque animai esse corpoream; enim, ubi (it) videtur propellere, corripere, mutare, regere ac versare; quorum nil videmus fieri posse sine tactu nec tactum sine corpore; nonne fatendumst animum animamque constare corporea natura?
- English skeleton: same; "it is seen to drive / snatch / change / steer and turn"; none of which we see can happen without touch, nor touch without body; must we not admit...
- MATCH.

168-169
- Latin skeleton: praeterea cernis animum pariter fungi cum corpore et una consentire.
- English skeleton: further, you see the mind is acted on together with the body and feels along with it.
- MATCH.

170-176
- Latin skeleton: si minus offendit vitam vis teli adacta (ossibus ac nervis disclusis, abl. abs.); at tamen insequitur languor, petitus terrae suavis, aestus mentis qui gignitur in terra, interdum incerta voluntas exsurgendi; ergo necessest naturam animi esse corpoream, quoniam laborat telis ictuque.
- English skeleton: if the weapon's force, though not striking out life, is driven in, laying open bones and sinews; yet faintness, pleasant sinking, turmoil born in the mind on the earth, now and then a wavering will to rise follow; therefore the mind's nature must be bodily, since it suffers from weapons and blow.
- MATCH. The protasis is recast concessively, but the sense is kept.

FINDINGS
227	AMBIGUITY	MINOR	0.50	"in toto corpore rerum" means throughout the whole body of these substances (the wine, perfume and savory body of 221-223), but "the whole body of things" reads, under this register's "nature/sum of things", as the body of the universe; "the savors and the scent throughout the whole body of these things."
