ANALYSIS
931-949. L: [si] natura mittat (pres subj) vocem et increpet (pres subj) alicui nostrum; speech: quid tibi tanto operest (indic), quod indulges (2sg pres) luctibus; quid congemis ac fles (2sg); nam si vita grata fuit (perf) et non commoda perfluxere atque interiere (perf, negated), cur non recedis ut conviva plenus et capis quietem securam (2sg pres); sin ea quae fructus es periere profusa vitaque in offensost, cur quaeris addere (quod pereat, occidat: rel subj) [et] non facis finem; nam nihil est quod machiner inveniamque (1sg subj), quod placeat; eadem sunt omnia; si corpus non marcet et artus non languent (negated), eadem tamen restant, si perges vincere saecla, potius si numquam sis moriturus. E: if nature should find a voice and rebuke one of us; what ails you that you give yourself up; why groan and weep; for if life was welcome and goods have not run through and perished, why not withdraw and take rest; but if what you enjoyed is lost and life offends, why seek to add more, only to be lost again, and not make an end; there is nothing I can devise to please; if body not yet withering and limbs not slack, still all the same, if you go on to outlast generations, more so if never to die. MATCH.
950-951. L: quid respondemus (1pl), nisi naturam intendere (inf) litem iustam et exponere causam veram verbis. E: what do we answer except that nature presses a just suit and sets out a true case in her words. MATCH.
952-962. L: si grandior seniorque queratur et lamentetur obitum (subj), non merito inclamet magis et increpet (subj, rhetorical question); speech: aufer lacrimas, compesce querellas (imper); perfunctus praemia marces (2sg); quia aves quod abest, temnis praesentia, vita elapsast inperfecta ingrataque, et mors adstitit (perf) nec opinanti ante quam possis discedere satur ac plenus; nunc mitte omnia aliena aetate (imper), concede magnis necessis. E: if someone older should complain and lament, would she not cry louder and rebuke; away with tears, check complaints; you have had the prizes and wither; since you crave the absent and scorn the present, life slipped away unfinished; death, unlooked for, stands at your head before you can depart full; let go of what does not suit your age; yield to greater ones, you must. MATCH (tamen at 961 unexpressed; text crux noted).
963-965. L: iure agat, increpet, incilet (subj); cedit (pres) vetustas extrusa novitate; necesse est reparare aliud ex aliis. E: rightly she would plead, rebuke, reproach; the old, thrust out by newness, gives way; one thing must be restored from others. MATCH.
966-971. L: nec quisquam deditur in barathrum nec Tartara (neg); materies opus est ut crescant (purpose) saecla; quae omnia (vita perfuncta, neut pl with omnia) te sequentur (fut); ergo ante haec quam tu cecidere cadentque; alid ex alio numquam desistet oriri; vita datur mancipio nulli, usu omnibus. E: no one handed into the Pit or Tartarus; matter needed so later generations may grow; they all, their life lived through, will follow you; no less than you these have fallen and will fall; one thing never ceases to rise from another; life given to none to own, to all to use. MATCH.
972-977. L: respice quam nil ad nos fuerit (perf subj) vetustas ante acta temporis aeterni quam nascimur; hoc speculum natura exponit futuri temporis post mortem; numquid horribile apparet, num triste videtur quicquam, non securius exstat omni somno. E: look back how the age gone by before we are born was nothing to us; this mirror nature holds up of time to come after death; does anything horrible appear, seem grim; is it not more untroubled than sleep. MATCH.
978-979. L: ea quae prodita sunt esse Acherunte, in vita sunt omnia nobis. E: whatever things are told to exist in Acheron are all here in our life. MATCH.
980-983. L: nec Tantalus timet saxum inpendens aere (neg), torpens formidine cassa; sed metus divom inanis urget mortalis, timent casum quem ferat fors. E: no Tantalus fears the rock hanging in the air, numb with empty dread; rather empty fear of the gods oppresses mortals, they fear the fall chance may bring. MATCH.
984-991. L: nec volucres ineunt Tityon iacentem, nec possunt reperire quicquam quod scrutentur perpetuam aetatem; quamlibet exstet proiectu immani, qui optineat (subj; qui = Tityos) non novem iugera sed orbem terrai, non tamen poterit perferre dolorem nec praebere cibum semper. E: nor do birds enter Tityos lying; nor can they find anything to search for through eternity; however vast his sprawl, though "it" (the body) should cover not nine acres but the earth, he could not bear eternal pain nor furnish food forever. MATCH (subject of optineat shifted to body, sense kept).
992-994. L: Tityos nobis hic est, quem iacentem in amore volucres lacerant, exest angor, scindunt curae cuppedine. E: Tityos is here, the one lying in love whom birds tear, anguish eats, cares rend. MATCH.
995-1002. L: Sisyphus est ante oculos, qui imbibit petere fasces secures, et recedit victus tristisque; nam petere imperium quod inanest nec datur, et sufferre laborem, hoc est trudere saxum monte adverso nixantem, quod volvitur rusum et petit aequora campi. E: Sisyphus is here, who seeks the rods and axes, thirsting for them, and withdraws beaten and grim; to seek power, empty and never given, and endure labor, is to push a rock uphill which rolls back and rushes for the plain. MATCH.
1003-1010. L: deinde pascere naturam animi ingratam semper, explere, satiare numquam, quod faciunt tempora annorum cum redeunt ferunt, nec explemur fructibus, hoc est quod memorant puellas congerere laticem in vas pertusum, quod expleri nulla ratione potestur. E: always to feed the ungrateful nature of the mind, fill it and never satisfy it, as the seasons do when they return and bring offspring, and yet we are never filled, this is the girls who pour water into a vessel full of holes, which can by no means be filled. MATCH.
1011-1023. L: Cerberus, Furiae, lucis egestas, Tartarus eructans; qui neque sunt usquam nec possunt esse; sed in vita est metus poenarum pro male factis insignis insignibus, luela sceleris, carcer, iactus, verbera...; quae etsi absunt, mens conscia adhibet stimulos torretque flagellis, nec videt qui terminus possit nec quae finis, et metuit ne gravescant in morte; hic vita stultorum fit Acherusia. E: Cerberus, Furies, lack of light, Tartarus belching; these are nowhere and cannot be; in life fear of punishment, notable as deeds are notable, atonement: prison, hurling, scourges...; even if absent, mind conscious applies goads, scorches with whips, does not see what limit can be nor what end, fears they may grow heavier in death; here life of fools becomes an Acheron. MATCH.
1024-1052. L: hoc dicere possis tibi; [speech] Ancus reliquit lumina oculis (sis); qui melior fuit; inde reges occiderunt qui imperitarunt; ille ipse qui stravit viam, dedit iter, docuit ire, contempsit murmura, lumine adempto animam fudit; Scipiadas ossa dedit terrae; adde repertores, adde comites, quorum unus Homerus sceptra potitus sopitus quietest eadem; Democritum postquam vetustas admonuit motus languescere, caput obvius optulit; Epicurus obit, qui superavit et restinxit stellas ut sol exortus; tu dubitabis et indignabere obire, cui vita mortua est vivo, qui conteris partem maiorem somno, stertis vigilans, nec cessas cernere somnia, geris mentem sollicitam, nec potes reperire quid sit mali, cum urgeris curis et fluitans vagaris errore. E: same clauses, same subjects, tenses and negations; sis unexpressed; "wavering" transferred from animo to error. MATCH.
