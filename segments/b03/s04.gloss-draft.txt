548-557
Latin: quoniam-clauses: mens (subj) est pars una, manet fixa loco certo; comparison: aures, oculi, alii sensus qui vitam cumque gubernant; vel uti manus/oculus/nares secreta nequeunt sentire neque esse, sed lincuntur (pres. pass. pl.) tempore tali. Main: sic animus non quit esse sine corpore et ipso homine; relative quod (corpus) vas illius esse videtur; sive aliud quid vis fingere coniunctius ei; quandoquidem corpus adhaeret conexu.
English: since mind is a part and stays fixed; as ears/eyes/senses steer life; since hand/eye/nostrils severed cannot feel or be, but "melt away"; so mind cannot be without body and man; which seems its vessel; or whatever you picture; since body clings with a bond.
DIFF: lincuntur ("are left, fail") rendered as liquuntur ("melt away"); the note covers only tali.

Latin: potestas corporis atque animi coniuncta valent fruunturque vita; nec natura animi sola potest edere motus vitalis sine corpore; nec corpus cassum anima potest durare et uti sensibus.
English: the same propositions, with "only" making the implication explicit.

580-591
Latin: cum corpus nequeat perferre discidium quin tabescat; quid dubitas quin vis animae emanarit coorta ex imo, diffusa uti fumus, atque corpus conciderit ruina; quia fundamenta (animae) mota sunt penitus loco et manant foras per artus, per flexus viarum, foramina. ut possis noscere naturam animae exisse dispertitam et prius distractam esse in corpore quam enaret in auras.
English: the body's ("its") foundations moved from their place, as the spirit flows outward through the limbs.
DIFF: fundamenta animae, the subject of both mota sunt and manant, becomes the body's foundations, and "the spirit" takes over manant.

English: the same, with "it" for the impersonal and "about to go" for ire.

FINDINGS
3.586	MISTRANSLATION	MODERATE	0.65	"fundamenta foras manant animaeque" makes the spirit's foundations the subject of both mota sunt and manant, but the English makes them the body's foundations ("its foundations") and gives manant to "the spirit"; fix 585-586: "in such great ruin, because, moved deep from their place, / the foundations of the spirit flow outward through the limbs"
3.646	LEXICAL	MINOR	0.60	"mobilitate mali" uses the register term mobilitas (= mobility, never "speed"), but the English has "swiftness"; fix: "through the mobility of the harm, cannot feel the pain;"
3.553	LEXICAL	MINOR	0.50	"lincuntur" as printed means "are left, fail" (as lincunt at 3.542), but "melt away" renders the emended liquuntur, and the note mentions only tali; fix: "but in such a case are lost in a little time," or extend the note to cover liquuntur
