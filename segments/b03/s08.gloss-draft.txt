ANALYSIS
1053-1059: Latin: si possent (subj. impf., pl.) homines noscere [e quibus causis id fiat; unde tanta mali moles constet], proinde ac videntur sentire pondus inesse animo [quod se (=homines) gravitate fatiget]; apodosis haut ita vitam agerent (subj. impf.), ut nunc videmus [quisque nescire quid sibi velit, et quaerere semper commutare locum, quasi onus deponere possit (sg.)]. English: if men could also know the causes and source, just as they plainly feel a weight that wears them out; they would hardly live as we see them, each not knowing, always seeking to change place, as if he could set down the burden. MATCH ("plainly feel" for videntur sentire and "hardly" for haut are within range).
1060-1062: Latin: exit ille [quem pertaesumst esse domi], revertit; quippe qui sentiat foris nihilo melius esse. English: he goes out, the man sick of home, comes back, since he feels no better off outside. MATCH.
1063-1067: Latin: currit agens mannos, instans quasi ferre auxilium tectis ardentibus (dat. pl.); oscitat cum tetigit limina; aut abit in somnum gravis, oblivia quaerit; aut properans urbem petit atque revisit. English: same clauses, same subject throughout. MATCH.
1068-1070: Latin: quisque se fugit; at quem effugere haut potis est; [subject quisque] ingratius haeret et odit (two verbs, one subject), propterea quia aeger causam morbi non tenet. English: each runs from himself; that self he cannot escape; it (the self) clings to him against his will, and he hates it; because he does not grasp the cause. DIFF: subject of haeret moved from the man to the self.
1071-1075: Latin: quam si bene videat (subj.), rebus relictis (abl. abs.) quisque iam studeat cognoscere naturam rerum, quoniam ambigitur status temporis aeterni non unius horae, in quo sit mortalibus omnis aetas quae post mortem restat manendo. English: if he saw it, each would leave his affairs and study the nature of things, since the state of eternal time is in question, in which mortals must spend all the age remaining after death. MATCH (iam unrendered, slight).
1076-1077: Latin: quae mala tanta cupido vitai (subj.) nos subigit (ind.) trepidare in dubiis periclis? English: what evil, what great craving for life compels us to tremble amid doubtful dangers? MATCH.
1078-1079: Latin: finis vitae mortalibus adstat; nec letum devitari pote quin obeamus. English: a fixed end stands waiting; death cannot be avoided, we must meet it. MATCH ("fixed" slight addition).
1080-1081: Latin: versamur ibidem atque insumus usque; nec ulla nova voluptas procuditur vivendo. English: matches. MATCH.
1082-1084: Latin: dum abest quod avemus, id videtur exsuperare cetera; post, cum illud contigit, aliud avemus; sitis aequa tenet [nos] vitai semper hiantis. English: matches. MATCH.
1085-1086: Latin: in dubio est quam fortunam vehat postera aetas, quid ferat casus, qui exitus instet. English: matches. MATCH.
1087-1089: Latin: nec vitam ducendo demimus hilum de tempore mortis, nec delibare valemus quo minus possimus esse diu perempti. English: matches. MATCH.
1090-1094: Latin: licet condere saecla quod vis vivendo; mors aeterna illa manebit nihilo minus; nec minus diu iam non erit ille qui ex hodierno lumine finem vitai fecit, et ille qui multis mensibus annisque ante occidit. English: matches ("for you" with manebit is light expansion). MATCH.
