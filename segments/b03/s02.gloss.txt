ANALYSIS
262-265: L: primordia (subj) cursant (pres ind act pl) inter se motibus; result ut: nihil unum possit secernier (pres pass inf), nec potestas divisa fieri spatio (neg); sed (subj they) extant quasi multae vis unius corporis. E: first-beginnings run among one another; so that no single one can be set apart, nor its power be made separate by space; but they stand out as the many forces of one body. MATCH ("quasi" thinly kept by "as").
266-272: L: quod genus: in viscere est odor, color, sapor; tamen ex his omnibus est unum augmen; sic calor, aer, venti potestas mixta creant (pres pl) unam naturam, et (with) mobilis illa vis, quae dividit (sg) initum motus ollis, unde oritur sensifer motus. E: same, with "together with" for et; force hands out the beginning of motion to them; from which sense-bearing motion arises. MATCH.
273-275: L: haec natura latet subestque; nec quicquam est infra hac (neg); atque est anima animae totius. E: same. MATCH.
276-281: L: quod genus: in membris mixta latens vis animi est potestasque animae, quia creata est (sg) de corporibus parvis paucisque; sic haec vis nominis expers, facta minutis corporibus, latet, est quasi anima animae, dominatur corpore toto. E: same. MATCH.
282-287: L: necesse est ventus, aer, calor vigeant (subj pl) commixta per artus, aliud aliis subsit magis emineatque; ut unum videatur fieri ab omnibus; ni calor, ventus, potestas aeris seorsum interemant sensum solvantque diducta. E: same, "lest" for ni. MATCH.
288-293: L: est animo calor quem sumit in ira cum fervescit et ardor micat; est frigida aura, comes formidinis, quae ciet horrorem et concitat artus; est status aeris pacati, qui fit pectore tranquillo voltuque sereno. E: same. MATCH.
294-298: L: plus calidi est illis quibus corda acria et mens effervescit; quo genere est vis leonum, qui rumpunt pectora fremitu gementes nec possunt capere fluctus irarum. E: same. MATCH.
299-301: L: mens cervorum frigida est magis ventosa et concitat auras gelidas, quae faciunt motum tremulum existere. E: same. MATCH.
302-306: L: natura boum vivit magis placido aere; nec fax irai subdita percit (neg, umquam) nimis, suffundens umbra caliginis; nec torpet perfixa telis pavoris (neg); sita est inter utrasque. E: same. MATCH.
307-309: L: quamvis doctrina constituat (subj) quosdam politos pariter, tamen illa (doctrina) relinquit vestigia prima naturae animi. E: same. MATCH.
310-313: L: nec putandum est (neg) mala posse evelli radicitus quin (= so as to prevent) hic decurrat, ille temptetur, ille tertius accipiat (three subj under one quin). E: nor must we think faults can be torn out, so that one man does not run...; another be assailed...; a third take... DIFF: the negation is written into the first clause only, so the second and third read as positive results.
314-318: L: necesse est naturas hominum differre moresque; quorum causas nequeo exponere nec reperire tot nomina figurarum quot sunt principiis, unde oritur variantia. E: same. MATCH.
319-322: L: video posse firmare illud: vestigia naturarum linqui usque adeo parvola, quae ratio nequeat depellere, ut nihil inpediat degere vitam dignam dis. E: same. MATCH.
323-326: L: haec natura tenetur ab omni corpore, est custos et causa salutis; nam haerent radicibus communibus nec videntur posse divelli sine pernicie. E: same. MATCH.
327-330: L: quod genus: evellere odorem haud facile est quin natura intereat; sic extrahere naturam animi animaeque haud facile est quin omnia dissoluantur. E: same. MATCH.
331-336: L: abl. abs. principiis inplexis; fiunt praedita vita consorti; nec quaeque potestas corporis atque animi videtur posse sentire seorsum sine vi alterius; sed sensus accensus conflatur motibus communibus. E: same. MATCH.
337-338: L: corpus per se nec gignitur nec crescit neque videtur durare post mortem. E: same. MATCH.
339-343: L: non, ut umor dimittit vaporem datum neque convellitur ipse sed manet incolumis, sic artus relicti possunt perferre discidium animai; sed pereunt convulsi putrescuntque. E: same. MATCH.
344-347: L: ex ineunte aevo mutua contagia (subj, nom pl) corporis atque animai (gen) discunt (pres pl) vitalis motus (acc pl obj), reposta (with contagia) membris maternis; ut discidium nequeat fieri sine peste. E: the body and the spirit (subj) learn the mutual contacts (obj) of the motions of life (gen); laid away (attached to body and spirit); so that separation cannot happen. DIFF: subject and object swapped; genitive misattached.
348-349: L: ut videas, quoniam causa salutis coniuncta est, naturam eorum consistere coniunctam. E: same ("must" slight). MATCH.
350-353: L: si quis refutat corpus sentire atque credit animam permixtam suscipere motum quem sensum nominamus, repugnat contra res manifestas. E: same. MATCH.
354-355: L: quis adferet quid sit corpus sentire, si non quod res ipsa dedit ac docuit? E: same. MATCH.
356-358: L: (objection) abl. abs. anima dimissa, corpus caret sensu. perdit enim quod non proprium fuit in aevo, multaque perdit cum expellitur aevo. E: same. MATCH.
359-361: L: dicere oculos nullam rem cernere posse, sed animum spectare per eos ut foribus reclusis, difficile est, cum sensus eorum ducat contra. E: same. MATCH.
362-364: L: sensus trahit atque detrudit ad acies ipsas, praesertim cum nequimus cernere fulgida, quia lumina luminibus praepediuntur. E: same. MATCH.
365-366: L: quod non fit foribus; neque ostia reclusa, qua cernimus, suscipiunt laborem. E: same. MATCH.
367-369: L: si lumina sunt pro foribus, animus videtur debere cernere magis oculis exemptis, postibus sublatis. E: same. MATCH.
370-373: L: nequaquam possis sumere quod sententia Democriti ponit: primordia corporis atque animi singula, adposita alternis, variare ac nectere membra. E: same. MATCH.
374-380: L: cum elementa animae sunt minora quam quibus corpus constat, tum numero concedunt et rara dissita sunt, ut possis promittere: quantula queant corpora prima iniecta ciere motus sensiferos, tanta intervalla tenere exordia prima animai. E: same. MATCH.

FINDINGS
345	GRAMMAR	MODERATE	0.80	"corporis atque animai mutua vitalis discunt contagia motus" has the mutual contacts of body and spirit (contagia, nominative, with reposta in 346) learning the vital motions (accusative), but the English makes "the body and the spirit" the subject that learns "the mutual contacts of the motions of life" (see also 344 and its stray comma); fix 344-345: "So from the beginning of life the mutual contacts / of body and spirit learn the motions of life,"
311	AMBIGUITY	MODERATE	0.55	the single "quin" in 311 governs "decurrat", "temptetur" and "accipiat" (311-313), but the English writes the negation into 311 alone ("does not run"), so "another be assailed... a third take" in 312-313 can be read as positive results of tearing out the faults; fix 311-313: "so as to keep one man from running more readily to fierce anger, / another from being assailed a little sooner by fear, a third / from taking certain things more mildly than is right."
